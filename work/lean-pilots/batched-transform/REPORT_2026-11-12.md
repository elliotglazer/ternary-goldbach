# Kernel-only transform-stage experiment

The exact-statement comparison improves the previous four arithmetic certificates
by **1.40x** in median fresh kernel replay. The stage experiments do not show a
large gain from putting more arithmetic in one theorem. A balanced assembly does
let a 256-butterfly stage pass at the default recursion limit where flat assembly
fails. All four exported real-error theorems use exactly `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

This is an arithmetic benchmark and reusable checked stage implementation. It is
not an L-function evaluation, a complete FFT, a zero-location certificate, or an
end-to-end Goldbach proof. No replacement of PlattGRH is claimed.

## What the theorem checks

At precision p, S=2^p. Each input consists of signed integers representing two
complex values u,v and a coefficient w, all on grid 1/S. The intended outputs
are u+w*v and u-w*v. The implementation uses the three-product identity for
complex multiplication, followed by nearest rounding with positive half ties.
The integer residual checker validates both complex outputs, source and output
ranges, and raw product ranges. It rejects mismatched list lengths.

`StageCheck.lean` proves that every accepted row satisfies the four scaled
residual inequalities. `StageReal.lean` proves their real-number interpretation:
every real and imaginary output coordinate differs from its exact dyadic
butterfly value by at most **1/(2S)**. `CertifiedReal.lean` discharges all numeric
premises and exports the full stage statements. These are unconditional theorems
about their specified literal data, not generic results whose numerical
acceptance is left as a hypothesis.

The stage fixtures are:

| Fractional bits | Butterflies | Complex outputs | Coordinate error bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|100|64|128|2^-101|
|343|64|128|2^-344|
|415|64|128|2^-416|
|343|256|512|2^-344|

Input coordinates have magnitude at most 1024; output coordinates at most 4096.
Thus input integer representations allow p+10 magnitude bits, plus sign. These
are representative bounded data, not the maximum integer parts of every
production branch. The 343- and 415-bit choices cover fractional widths appearing
in the previous small-conductor cost plan. They do not establish a conversion
rate for every differently scaled production operand.

Python Decimal trigonometry chooses varied approximate FFT coefficients, using
180 decimal digits, and Python integer arithmetic generates rounded outputs.
Neither computation is trusted for theorem acceptance. Lean verifies the exact
dyadic transform defined by the supplied coefficients. **Their proximity to the
intended roots of unity is not certified here.** Likewise the synthetic input
values are not certified enclosures of Hurwitz coefficients or spectral values.
Root uncertainty, source uncertainty, multistage propagation, and connection to
the actual character/time transforms remain additional work.

## Matched results

These are elapsed fresh kernel replay times, excluding imports, elaboration,
and proof-pool preparation. Every row is the median of three process runs.
Stage runs repeat the complete certificate closure 10 times per process;
the legacy comparison repeats it 50 times. Generated proof helper theorems
are included. Source and shared generic theorems are checked separately.

| Certificate design | Object | Median ms | Observed min-max ms |

|Direct certificates, 8 declarations|**Exactly the same four propositions**|23.39|19.51-24.21|
|One residual certificate per butterfly|64 butterflies, p100|176.95|160.95-178.27|
|One residual certificate per butterfly|64 butterflies, p343|176.29|173.12-178.03|
|One residual certificate per butterfly|64 butterflies, p415|191.88|173.16-197.27|

|Direct integer rounding, whole stage|64 butterflies, p343|198.47|180.74-214.44|

The old-to-direct comparison reduces median replay time by 28.8%, a 1.40x
throughput gain. All four original proposition texts were copied unchanged.
The old 21-declaration-per-fixture proofs are replayed in full. The new proofs
compute the final propositions directly. Their axiom audits are actually empty,
which also satisfies the standard-three-axiom requirement.

For the 64-butterfly stages, direct integer rounding improves the whole-stage
encoding by about 1.46x. The same method is only about 1.08x faster than the
already better per-butterfly residual encoding. That latter difference is small
relative to measurement variation and is not a robust performance claim.

`StageFast.checkFast_implies_checkOne` proves that, for the positive even scale,
the direct-rounding checker implies the original check, including its numerical
range conditions. This comparison does not drop those obligations. Both paths
feed the same real-error theorem.

The larger stage uses 48 replayed declarations including helper proofs and
assembly. `TreeBinding.lean` separately proves that the balanced input and output
lists equal the original flat lists. No input or output was omitted to make
assembly fit. Its median is about **3.19 ms per butterfly**. The best observed
64-butterfly median is about **2.55 ms per butterfly**. These are different sizes
and representations, not evidence of a single size-independent throughput.

## Trust and measurement controls

One complete rebuild of the 14 owned modules needed by the four exported real
bounds used **75.69 CPU seconds** and **77.76 seconds of driver wall time**.
Peak single-process working set was **1.03 GB**. This includes generic checker
proofs, the real interpretation, all four datasets, numerical certificates,
balanced-list binding, imports, and serialization. It excludes compilation of
the existing toolchain/Mathlib dependencies and Python fixture generation. It is
a single full-build measurement, not a repeated build-time comparison.

- Lean 4.35.0, direct executable, `-t0 -j1 -M4096`; no dependency builds.
- Default heartbeat and recursion settings. Replay reports trust 0, 200000000
  kernel heartbeat units and recursion depth 512. No limits were raised in Lean.
- Four final real-error theorem audits return exactly the standard three axioms.
  No `sorry`, problem-specific axiom, or native proof acceptance is used.
- `Controls.lean` submits true and false Boolean equalities directly to
  `Kernel.Environment.addDeclCore`. Both valid controls pass. Six corruptions
  fail, covering rounded output, length, order, coefficient, and direct-rounding
  paths. Positive and negative half ties also have checked concrete examples.
- Each timed repetition begins from the same base environment. The numerical
  theorem closure is renamed to fresh names and checked in dependency order;
  any remaining reference to its original namespace causes failure. Shared
  literal definitions and proved generic lemmas remain base dependencies.
- Windows process CPU times, wall times, memory counters and source/object hashes
  are recorded separately. The replay loop measures elapsed time. Other machine
  activity was not excluded, so observed ranges matter.

The first exploratory replay accidentally omitted helper proofs generated by
`decide +kernel`. It measured cheap theorem wrappers and is invalid as arithmetic
timing. The `replay_*10016_01` and `_02` logs are retained to document that error.
All `timed_*` successful receipts use recursive helper collection. In particular,
the exact-statement legacy closure contains all 84 original declarations, so the
previous arithmetic calibration was not invalidated by this discovery.

Flat 256-row proof assembly and an initial reflexivity proof of flat/tree equality
hit the default recursion limit. Balanced assembly and kernel-computed list
equality resolved these failures without increasing the limit. Exploratory failed
sources and logs remain outside the final successful module set.

## Consequence for the Goldbach plan

Use the compact direct certificate as the measured successor for the four old
calibration propositions. Retain small arithmetic certificates and balanced
assembly as the working stage architecture. A single large Boolean certificate
is not automatically the efficient choice in Lean.

The weak dependence on fractional width in these samples is consistent with
substantial term-reduction overhead, although this is not a phase attribution
profile and does not prove that large-integer multiplication is always cheap.
The measurements justify further investigation of integer representation,
packed arithmetic, and specialized reduction code. They do not establish that
any of those approaches will deliver another particular speedup.

**Do not divide the old end-to-end Fermi estimates by 1.40, 1.46, or a ratio
between the old fixture and a new butterfly.** The previous mapping from workload
macro-equivalents to a 21-declaration fixture remains unvalidated. The new stage
does not include roots, coefficient generation, analytic error propagation,
global scheduling, zero counting, or binary Goldbach coverage. This experiment
has measured a local improvement and a scalable assembly method, not a reduction
in the complete proof's certified compute bill.

## Files and reproduction

`SUMMARY.json` contains all successful final replay receipts and artifact hashes.
`axioms_final.log` and `controls01.log` record the trust and corruption checks.
`final_build_closure.json` records the complete own-source build of the four
exported real bounds, including generic arithmetic and real interpretation.
Imported Mathlib/toolchain build costs are excluded.

Run `reproduce.py unique_label` with the available Python installation to rebuild
all successful configurations and repeat the comparisons. Run
`build_closure.py another_unique_label` for only the complete exported proof
closure. Both call `run_lean.py`, preserve existing log names, and rebuild only
owned modules. The pinned executable and existing Mathlib cache paths are explicit
in that runner. `suite.py` and `final_suite.py` are historical exploration drivers,
not the successful reproduction entry point.
