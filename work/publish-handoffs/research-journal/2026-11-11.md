Our progress so far supports improvements within the existing strategy. It has not revealed an almost-complete mathematical breakthrough that would remove its dominant obligations. I would be overstating things if I suggested another round of planning was likely to uncover one.

The most promising research question is **whether the analytic argument can consume a cheaper aggregate certificate instead of demanding detailed information about each character and its zeros.** But that only saves compute if proving the aggregate certificate costs substantially less. Moving the same work into another theorem accomplishes little.

I therefore recommend committing to the conservative Lean strategy while keeping a small, bounded research effort on that question. Complete implementations will also tell us which obligation actually dominates, making any subsequent mathematical research much better targeted.
