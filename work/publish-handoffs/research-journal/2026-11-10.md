**QRH is least suitable where the proof needs very small, explicit errors from low-lying zeros at a fixed numerical threshold.** That is precisely where bounded RH is strongest.

1. **Pointwise major-arc estimates near the starting threshold.**

An explicit-formula term contains \(x^\rho\). Holding its other factors fixed, replacing \(x^{1/2}\) by \(x^{7/8}\) costs \(x^{3/8}\), approximately **\(10^{10}\)** at \(x=4.9\cdot10^{26}\). The current error budget cannot absorb that. This is the most serious mismatch.

2. **Uniform estimates over many characters and additive twists.**

QRH restricts zero locations; it does not itself supply cancellation between their contributions or across characters. Taking absolute values and then summing over characters can be expensive. Smoothing suppresses high zeros, but leaves the low zeros, where we have just surrendered critical-line control. The existing major-arc \(L^2\) proof depends on these quantitative estimates.

3. **Tight prime-counting, Mertens and Möbius bounds over finite or intermediate ranges.**

A schematic relative error \(C x^{-1/8}\) eventually becomes small, but that does not show it is small enough throughout a prescribed range. Constants and starting points matter. Raising the starting point leaves a gap that our finite Goldbach input does not automatically cover.

Larger individual errors also worsen cross terms. Averaging might recover enough, but QRH alone does not furnish the required mixed fourth-moment estimate. That is a separate analytic obligation, currently open in our work.

QRH is better suited to **eventual estimates where the starting threshold can be large**, or arguments that only need some fixed power saving. Our far-asymptotic prime-estimate drafts exploit that distinction.

For the full Goldbach proof, the difficult question is therefore not just how to import QRH into Lean. It is how to recover sufficiently sharp **low-height, finite-scale control** through new averaging estimates, sharper analysis, or separately proved finite certificates.
