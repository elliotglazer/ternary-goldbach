# Prime ladders, finite-height counts and precision of Hardy amplitudes

The Lean source contains a prime ladder through (10^{27}). The HOL4 work proves several implications needed for finite-height root counting. Native interval computations separately demonstrate that sparse Hurwitz amplitudes can recover hidden sign changes at two conductors. The remaining analytic obligations and the difference between a sampled cost and a campaign budget matter when interpreting these results.

## Prime ladder and cost interpretation

`TernaryGoldbach.Ladder.ladder_prime` supplies a prime in each of 254,638,308 windows. The last window reaches beyond (10^{27}), and the consecutive-prime gap guarantee is below (4\cdot10^{18}-4). The assembly imports 128 data groups. Replacing the binary verification range by (3\cdot10^{17}) or (10^{16}) would require a corresponding stronger gap guarantee. This investigation inspected the declaration and group assembly. A fresh Lean kernel replay and a costed denser ladder remain open. [Lean theorem](../ternary-goldbach/TernaryGoldbach/Ladder/Main.lean) · [Data assembly](../ternary-goldbach/TernaryGoldbach/Ladder/Data.lean)

If a plan requires 214,000 **vCPU-hours**, 300 continuously occupied vCPUs imply a minimum of 29.7 days. A $10,000 ceiling leaves at most $0.04673 per effective vCPU-hour, including other charges. These figures are conditional arithmetic. A 95th-percentile completion time under a hard spending cap requires a distribution for success before exhaustion. If exhaustion before completion has probability greater than 5%, that percentile is infinite when unfinished runs have infinite duration.
