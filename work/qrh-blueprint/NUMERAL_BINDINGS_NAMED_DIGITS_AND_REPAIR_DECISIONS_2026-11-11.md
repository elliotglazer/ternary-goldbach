## Repair decisions follow the failed certificate inequality

Accept the reviewed precision-repair policy. Producer-contract failure, failure of an actual strict sign and failure of count closure are separate events. Increasing transform p changes only its allocated error contribution unless the other producers are also refined. At fixed other tolerances its sufficient output radius is R_fixed+135*C_T*2^-p. This bound cannot meet a target below its retained R_fixed by changing p alone. R_fixed is an upper allowance, not a lower bound on actual error.

Changing m reduces the omitted-sample allowance without reducing the Fourier allowance. Changing h affects both tails and kernel values. A genuinely finer coarse lattice changes Nyquist and the sample/guard costs; merely refining target offsets on the old lattice does not. Every new target set requires its exact dependency union and admitted source domains. Direct point evaluation can avoid this interpolation tail under its own complete contract.

Every retained sign must pass its actual |center|>radius test. When a true-value margin mu is separately known, 2r<mu is sufficient; r<mu alone is not. This corrects any weaker interpretation of older informal margin wording. At a true zero the strict test cannot succeed. Opposite signs establish a critical lower count, not exact multiplicity, and even multiplicity can be missed by signs. Central and inverse-channel conventions remain unchanged.

Finite-RH closure retains the strict pair-deficit inequality h_T*(K+2)-Abar+W-2*Bbar>0 with its actual critical evidence and canonical integral. It is not an exact zero-list claim. More value bits help only when they produce useful admissible evidence. Generation pays failed attempts; checking may retain the successful dependency union, with exact bindings, reusable objects and cache lifetimes accounted for. No repair frequency or unconditional termination is assumed.
