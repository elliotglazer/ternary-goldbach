For a primitive nonprincipal character chi of conductor c>1, retain the actual normalized completion

    f(s)=c^((s+e)/2) completedLFunction(chi,s),

with its actual parity e. Its positive-half-plane Gamma expression differs by at most a nonzero constant from conventions moving the parity factor outside the conductor power. Such a constant cancels from both f'/f and the unit-shift norm ratio. The source completion, rather than a totalized Gamma product at its poles, is the entire function used in the contour proof.

The finite contour producer supplies precisely the approximation (F) consumed by the quantitative note. For every compact disk K centered at 1/2 and every epsilon>0, there is a finite carrier Z of actual zeros with their exact positive analytic orders, containing all zeros in K, such that

    |Re(f'/f)(s) - sum_(rho in Z) m(rho) Re(1/(s-rho))| <= epsilon

at every nonzero point of f in K. The error is uniform even near the removed zeros. Its proof subtracts two finite residue identities at s and 1-conj(s), uses the actual same-character reflection, and takes good squares whose boundary error tends to zero under the source polynomial growth bound. Factorization is anchored at 2, not at a possibly vanishing central value. No RH, QRH, simplicity or central-nonvanishing premise is introduced.

The producer's last strict contour bound needs the reviewer's qualification when the radius M is zero. Use the non-strict bound, or enlarge the working radius to max(M,1). This preserves (F) on every compact disk. The horizontal application already uses positive radius. This supplement consumes the qualified statement and leaves the frozen author bytes unchanged.

Finite zero removal and the independently reviewed scalar J bound give

    integral_(1/2)^(3/2) [log|f(sigma+it)|-log|f(sigma+1+it)|] d sigma
       >= -4 Re(f'/f)(3/2+it).

The proof first obtains the right side minus 5epsilon and then lets epsilon tend to zero. It does not need an infinite Hadamard product or an interchange of an infinite zero sum and an integral. The producer's optional infinite identities are unnecessary for this consumer.
