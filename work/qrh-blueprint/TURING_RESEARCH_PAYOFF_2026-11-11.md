## Exact comparison and pricing scope

Let M_i be the frozen main count in operation category i and C_i the count for one extra common height across all large conductors. The inventory counts

    A_i(h) = (64h/5) C_i,
    T_i(h) = M_i + A_i(h).

The common grid step is 5/64. All six source rows are checked against these formulas. The source JSON is pinned to SHA256 8BEAD899428DE523323FCA7D833ECE6F4C5443213D32D4F32B964B18B218F7F9. It excludes conductor one, the small-conductor branch, interpolation, repair, phase/count certificates and seed generation.

For any fixed nonnegative prices p_i with positive total cost, the reduction in sum_i p_i T_i is a weighted average of the component reductions

    r_i(h_old,h_new) = [A_i(h_old)-A_i(h_new)]/T_i(h_old).

Indeed its weights are p_i T_i(h_old) divided by their sum. Thus it lies between the smallest and largest r_i without assuming that a complex multiplication costs the same as a negation. The fixed-price qualification matters: different operand widths, cache effects, proof representations, or conditional rounding precision can invalidate that pricing model. The exact counts remain valid but do not determine runtime.

The receipt records all rational fractions. For h200 to h50 the extreme fractions are

Both lie between .106 and .110. For h50 to zero they are

Both lie between .039 and .042. Removing half of h50 therefore saves less than 2.1% of the priced transform subtotal. Adding unchanged nonnegative setup costs would only decrease these percentages within that model.

This is not a 4.2% bound on the whole proof's possible improvement. Auxiliary signs, interpolation and repair can have different scaling. A new theorem might also simplify main-grid work, permit another backend or be useful elsewhere. Conversely the finite coverage and analytic foundation costs do not disappear when the auxiliary transform shrinks. The receipt prices none of them.

## Research decision

Retain the b30.25 argument as the current elementary fallback. Finish independent review of b18 because its finite contour machinery may have wider use and could reduce auxiliary work further. Do not equate a factor-of-two reduction in b with a factor-of-two reduction in overall proof cost. The larger possible savings still require progress on actual aggregate character estimates, finite coverage, and the complete certificate pipeline.

The independent source audit and review now support three conditional nonprincipal replacement targets: the direct plus energy, full mixed Wstar, and base-gap energy. Principal RH through 1e8 and the retained analytic and finite inputs remain. These targets are unproved.
