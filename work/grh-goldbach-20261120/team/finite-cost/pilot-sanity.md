# Can the finite proof fit the Saturday budget?

**The receipts rule out scaling the current per-shard process directly. A batched checker could still fit the budget. Its complete-block rate remains unmeasured.** Replacing the older arithmetic-only measurement with the two actual wide operators while retaining the model's other assumptions raises 51,541 to **60,285 useful core-hours**, or **$4,420 for workers**. The original $6,000 worker allocation then has 21,554 useful core-hours left. [Original model](deadline.json) · [Recalibration](reforecast.json)

Binary Goldbach through `4·10^15` contains 1,999,999,999,999,999 even targets. The model assigns 262,144 targets per tile, 16,384 tiles per full block, **7,629,394,532 tiles** and **465,662 blocks** overall. At the plan's $1.64224 per 32-core instance-hour and 70% useful utilization, a useful core-hour costs $0.0733143. The one-time shared prime table uses 14,340 modeled hours, or $1,051. The limits below subtract that table once. Each extra millisecond on every tile costs 2,119 useful core-hours or $155 before any extra allowance. The $6,000 row preserves $200 for the pilot, $1,800 for other costs and $2,000 of contingency. The last row is an absolute upper limit that leaves no funds for the pilot or other costs. The cheaper architecture needs to fit **all** recurring costs inside the middle columns, including certificates for each block's anchors, search, imports, semantic proof assembly, exceptions and output.

**The 16,384-target closed theorem took 227.81 CPU seconds.** It shared one 2,560-prime table and 79 anchor proofs across two shards. The two coverage processes alone used 34.09 CPU seconds, and a fresh table import used 15.80 CPU seconds. Repeating the entire job on equal-sized shards would use about **7.72 billion core-hours**. The table, imports and generic proofs could serve many more targets. On the real 262,144-target operands, two warm arithmetic equalities took **5.415 and 4.685 ms** each. The fresh arithmetic module processes used **5.031 and 3.500 CPU seconds** apiece. A process for every tile at their average would already use 9.04 million core-hours. The warm figures time serial kernel rechecks with a monotonic wall clock. The process receipts report Windows CPU time. **Semantic assembly is the clearest exposed cost.** A warm replay at width 8,192 took 139.31 and 107.70 ms for the two shard proofs, then 40.60 ms for their join. One average such application and half a join per *production-width* tile would alone use about **305,000 core-hours** at wall/CPU parity. That exceeds even the all-workers $10,000 upper limit. Treating a 5.05 ms operator as unavoidable and keeping the original rates for other work leaves **13.19 ms per tile** for additional Lean coverage under the $6,000 allocation. If those measured overheads stayed constant, an illustrative requirement would be `143.81/j + 15,800/k ≤ 13.19` in milliseconds, where one semantic operation with its share of the join handles `j` tiles and one cold import serves `k` tiles. Dedicating the entire remainder to one cost at a time requires at least **11 tiles per semantic operation** or **1,199 tiles per import**. Paying both requires more reuse. Adaptive batched proofs may have different costs. [Semantic replay](wide8192/assemblyReplay01/PackedAssemblyReplay.log)

The model budgets 130.16 ms per shared source prime, including certificate generation, for approximately 396.6 million roots. The small endpoint used 30.78 ms per marked source prime for building and assembling its source table. Its trial-divisor cutoff was only 169. The proposed block layout also allows **512 newly certified anchors for 16,384 tiles**, or one new root per 32 tiles. The wide fixture has **164 distinct anchors for two tiles**. Extending a common block-level anchor pool and a large prime table across all tiles is therefore essential. These measurements establish a target for the Linux full-block benchmark; the available receipts certify no complete block of 4,294,967,296 even targets. The new 262,144-width source expansion passed all 16 segment checks and their join in **106.125 CPU seconds**. It proves that the packed digits represent the supplied raw bitmap; prime certification is a separate stage. To illustrate the effect of the layout, paying that cost once for each of 16,384 tile positions and reusing each expansion across every block would cost about **483 core-hours**. Paying it once for every pair of production tiles would cost **112 million core-hours**. The checker still has to establish which expansions can be shared and measure the cost of importing them.
