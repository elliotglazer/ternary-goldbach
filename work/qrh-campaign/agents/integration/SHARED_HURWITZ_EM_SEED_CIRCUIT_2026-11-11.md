# A complete shared Euler–Maclaurin seed-jet circuit

2026-11-11. Research candidate, not an implemented evaluator or performance forecast. Only source reading, finite symbolic algebra and exact integer/rational bounds/counts were performed. No Hurwitz, trigonometric, exponential, prime or zero values were evaluated; no build or production run occurred.

The candidate computes all normalized alpha coefficients at one anchor from one collection of base powers. It replaces a per-coefficient Hurwitz oracle by explicit elementary leaves, finite recurrences and a uniform remainder. The counts below compare the original P24 mesh, independent P20 on the doubled mesh, and the reviewed shifted P27/P28 construction. They do not establish which complete implementation is fastest.

## 1. Source and function boundary

Platt's section4.1 describes a shared Hurwitz lattice and uses its alpha Taylor identity both in lattice construction and consumption. His stated historical parameters are not the parameters proposed here. The pinned 5.11 consumer reads precomputed lattice data; it is not proof that each historical anchor was independently generated by EM. This note supplies one defensible new baseline, not a reconstruction of the fastest historical seed generator. [Platt paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3087), [pinned consumer, lines29–38](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/djplatt/code/1f19fcbefa33d2fe5949ddbcd4d3df75deca6819/l-func/int-l-func5.11.cpp).

Fix s=1/2+it, |t|<=V<=10000, alpha in[0,1], z=1+alpha, and P<=32. The desired objects are

    C_k=(-1)^k(s)_k zeta(s+k,z)/k!,  0<=k<P.

These are normalized derivatives in alpha. The continued Hurwitz recurrence and derivative identity, rather than a critical-line series interchange, supply their semantics. The EM representation used below is the even-periodic-Bernoulli remainder form, obtained from finite Euler–Maclaurin and passage to the convergent remainder; it is equivalent by integration by parts to standard Hurwitz EM representations. [DLMF25.11(iii)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.11.iii), [derivative identity25.11.17](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.11.E17).

## 2. Exact finite formula and all-order remainder

Let N>=1, b=N+z, beta_r=B_(2r)/(2r)!, and w=s+k. The finite expression is

    zeta(w,z) = sum_(n=0)^(N-1) (n+z)^(-w)
              + b^(1-w)/(w-1) + b^(-w)/2
              + sum_(r=1)^m beta_r (w)_(2r-1)b^(1-w-2r)
              + R_m(w,b).

With the periodic Bernoulli polynomial evaluated on the real integration variable,

    R_m(w,b)=-(w)_(2m)/(2m)! *
             integral_(0,infinity) Btilde_(2m)(u)(b+u)^(-w-2m)du.

Here Re(w)+2m>1, b>0 and w!=1. These hold for every k, including k=0 and t=0; |s+k-1|>=1/2. No pole value or denominator-zero convention is needed.

The Fourier bound on the periodic Bernoulli polynomial and zeta(2m)<2 give

    |Btilde_(2m)(u)|/(2m)! <=4/(2pi)^(2m)<4/6^(2m).

Multiplying by |(s)_k|/k! joins the two rising products exactly into |(s)_(k+2m)|/k!. Set W=V+32+2m and choose N=ceil(W/3). Since b>=N+1 and |s+j|<=V+j+1<=W for all relevant factors,

    |C_k remainder|
      <=4*sqrt(b)/(k+2m-1/2) * (W/b)^k/k!
                                  * (W/(6b))^(2m)
      <=108*sqrt(N+2)/(2m-1/2) * 2^(-2m).                 (1)

The last step uses W/b<=3 and 3^k/k!<=exp(3)<27. The estimate is uniform over the complete real alpha interval and both signs of t. It bounds each actual coefficient in absolute norm, not only its weighted contribution to a later polynomial.

For m=64, use these common cutoffs for every P<=32:

| V | N |
|---:|---:|
|500|220|
|1000|387|
|2000|720|
|5000|1720|
|10000|3387|

Then N+2<4096, so (1)<2^-122. Even replacing sqrt(N+2) by N+2 gives the entirely rational bound below2^-116. Both leave room under a target coefficient radius eta=2^-100.

For a general requested eta>0, choose m and N=ceil((V+32+2m)/3) until

    108*(N+2)/(2m-1/2)*2^(-2m) <= eta/16.                 (2)

This exact rational search terminates: N grows linearly with m, whereas 2^(-2m) decays geometrically. The fixed m64 table is not an arbitrary-precision claim without this retuning.

## 3. Shared head and bounded correction recurrences

At a rational height and anchor all factors other than positive-base powers are rational complex numbers. Once per height compute exactly

    r_0=1, r_(k+1)=-r_k*(s+k)/(k+1).

For x=n+z, compute one elementary ball B_x=x^(-s), shared across k. Accumulate

    D_k=sum_(n<N) B_(n+z)*(n+z)^(-k),
    head_k=r_k*D_k.

Each term starts at B_x and obtains its next k value by multiplication by the positive rational1/x. This needs N(P-1) real-times-complex scales and NP complex accumulator additions per full-P anchor, followed by P output multiplications by r_k (the k=0 multiplication can be omitted). It does not evaluate P independent exponentials or logarithms for each n. Round the term recurrence to a common dyadic grid; then accumulation is exact integer addition on that grid, avoiding the product of N unrelated rational denominators.

For the endpoint, define

    E_l=(s)_l/(6b)^l, E_0=1,
    E_(l+1)=E_l*(s+l)/(6b),  l<P+2m-2,
    lambda_k=(-1)^k*6^k/k!,
    gamma_r=6^(2r)*beta_r.

The gamma_r are fixed exact signed rationals computed from the Bernoulli recurrence once, not numerical zeta values. Their signs are retained. The proof above gives |gamma_r|<4. The recurrence factor for E has norm<=1/2. The full endpoint coefficient is B_b*F_k, where

    F_k = b*lambda_k*E_k/(s+k-1) + lambda_k*E_k/2
          + (lambda_k/6)*sum_(r=1)^m gamma_r E_(k+2r-1).  (3)

This includes the pole-type term and all64 corrections. The largest E index is P+2m-2; confusing it with the remainder's rising-product length would omit a factor. All E entries are shared across k. The work is P+2m-2 contracting complex recurrence stages, mP rational-real scalings/additions for the correction sums, and O(P) endpoint assembly operations per anchor.

The normalization in (3) also gives useful conditioning bounds. Since |lambda_k|<729, |E_l|<=2^-l and |s+k-1|>=1/2,

    |F_k| <=27*(2b+1).                                   (4)

In particular the correction is not priced or bounded by a huge unscaled rising product whose cancellation is deferred to the end.

## 4. A full sufficient finite-precision allocation

The following makes the arithmetic part concrete for eta=2^-100, m64, N<=3387. It is deliberately conservative. General eta uses (2) and correspondingly recomputed bounds.

Let

    A=binom(V+P-1,P-1),   Fmax=27*(2*(N+2)+1),
    M=max(N*A,Fmax),
    delta=eta/(32M), u_D=eta/(32*N*A*P),
    u_C=eta/2^32, u_out=eta/32.

The exact r_k satisfy |r_k|<=A. Supply each B_x, including B_b, with absolute error<=delta and an initial dyadic center on the head term grid. Multiplication by1/x contracts because x>=1. After k term steps its error is at most delta+k*u_D. Consequently head accumulation and the final r_k multiplication, rounded with error<=u_out, contribute at most3eta/32.

For (3), round each E recurrence and each gamma_r product with complex error<=u_C, adding on a common grid exactly. The E errors are at most2u_C. Round the three final F components likewise. The total F error is bounded by

    [2916*(N+2)+729+69984+3]*u_C <2^24*u_C.

Here the69984 term is (729/6)*64*(8+1), accounting for both propagated E error and each rounded gamma product. The endpoint B_b*F_k therefore contributes at most eta/32+eta/128+eta/32 after its final rounding. One final head-plus-endpoint rounding adds eta/32. Adding the remainder<=eta/16 from (2) gives total error<eta. Every quoted rounding radius is a complex absolute error; coordinate grids must enforce this, not silently use a coordinate radius as a Euclidean radius.

This is a finite rational/dyadic circuit. Exact factors r_k,1/x,(s+l)/(6b),lambda_k,gamma_r and b/(s+k-1) require checked rational arithmetic, denominator positivity/nonzero proofs and ordinary rounding inequalities. Their bit costs are not zero. The long head never accumulates an expanding exact common denominator; the E recurrence also rounds at each contracting step. Only the short rational factor tables are exact.

For the height10000 band, conservative sufficient fractional-bit counts obtained from the exact bounds are:

| P | B_x absolute-error bits | Head recurrence-grid bits |
|---:|---:|---:|
|20|313|317|
|24|348|353|
|27|374|379|
|28|383|388|
|32|417|422|

These counts are not full mantissa sizes or measured backend precision. Integer parts, guards and elementary suballocations add their own requirements. They show why equal-cost seed slots are not a sound comparison: the unscaled high-order coefficients make P28 more demanding than P20/P24.

## 5. Elementary leaves and optional height sharing

Use the critical-line identity

    B_x=x^(-1/2)*cis(-t*log x),  1<=x<=3389.

Thus a leaf needs a certified real log, positive inverse square root, and unit-circle exponential; a general complex power oracle is unnecessary. For example bound log error by delta/(8*max(1,V)), amplitude error by delta/8 and the cis error at the approximate phase by delta/8. Real-phase Lipschitz continuity, amplitude<=1 and a final center rounding give a B_x ball of radius delta with slack. The finite log/cis series, range-reduction constants, square-root witnesses and their proofs remain actual work. Existing exact elementary-checker plans provide interfaces, not already generated values at these millions of arguments.

The rational x arguments lie on nested grids. Across the whole coarse-mesh schedule their union has27754497 members; on the doubled mesh it has55508993. Logs and inverse square roots can be certified once per argument at sufficient precision and reused across heights. This replaces repeated leaf generation by substantial persistent storage or streaming I/O; neither is free.

The fixed time step h=5/64 permits a further fully specified option. For each x, certify z_x=cis(-h*log x) once and initialize B_x at its first required height. Thereafter use rounded multiplication by z_x at each consecutive required height. The active argument sets grow with the five bands, so once introduced an argument remains required. For at most J=128000 updates, if the initial error is <=delta/4 and both step error and per-update rounding are <=delta/(16J),

    error_j <=(1+epsilon)^j*delta/4
               +j*(epsilon+u)*(1+epsilon)^(j-1) <delta.

The factor is bounded by2 since J*epsilon<1/2. New arguments are initialized at their actual first height; one must not silently start their recurrence at zero without paying earlier steps. This option needs at most two phase certificates per distinct argument (initial and step), rather than a cis evaluation at every height-argument pair, but adds one complex multiplication/rounding per subsequent pair and about21 fractional guard bits at the strictest band. It is a separate priced schedule, not automatic free reuse.

A global precision sufficient for the final band avoids invalid reuse of earlier wider balls. Adaptive recertification could save work but needs a new explicit ledger. Retaining tens of millions of phase states/log balls requires a memory or disk plan; sharding can trade storage for recomputation, and the model must charge that trade.

Their downstream residue contractions use P20; P24 retains its larger residue contraction bill. None of these arithmetic categories is equated with another by an unexplained constant cost.

A usable model therefore prices, per band and precision, (i) elementary log/root/cis certificates or the explicit recurrence alternative; (ii) exact rational factor tables; (iii) real-times-complex head scales and grid additions; (iv) E complex stages, Bernoulli terms and final products; (v) midpoint shifts; (vi) generation, serialization, replay and state I/O. Lean and ordinary MM0 must check the same mathematical circuit with their actual representation costs. A native numerical generator can propose the data but cannot replace its acceptance proofs.

Relative to independent fine P20, shifted P28 approximately halves the base-argument pair count and reduces head/correction operation counts, while requiring more bits. Relative to old-mesh P24 it keeps the same elementary argument count but raises precision and head work, then saves the known downstream P24-to-P20 contraction stages. Those competing terms—not a per-slot oracle price—decide whether the substitution wins. This note supplies a complete candidate circuit and symbolic cost inputs, not that measurement or an end-to-end winner.

## 7. Proof and implementation frontier

Required proofs are the actual Hurwitz EM identity and uniform remainder, the alpha-derivative/rising-product identity, signed Bernoulli data, exact positive-base power semantics, elementary leaf certificates, finite arithmetic/rounding soundness, and the finite sharing identities. The current campaign has reusable elementary and EM source plans, but this precise Hurwitz seed circuit and its closed data have not been implemented or accepted by a kernel. The old shifted-jet proof consumes the resulting coefficient balls only after these obligations are met.

The main deliberate limitations are common N/m rather than optimized per-order cutoffs, conservative absolute coefficient precision, and no fast multipoint seed algorithm. Platt's lattice propagation indicates that more sharing may exist. This baseline is useful precisely because those improvements can now be compared against explicit work rather than an unspecified seed oracle.
