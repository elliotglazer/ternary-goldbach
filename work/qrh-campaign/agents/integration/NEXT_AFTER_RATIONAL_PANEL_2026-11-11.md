## Bounded acceptance checklist

1. **Fixed meaning and well-formedness.** Bind the fixture inputs independently of certificate claims. Check ordered endpoints, positive rational denominators, nonnegative radii, typed constants, all operand indices earlier than their uses, initial theta3 and every type transition. Reject malformed/empty intervals before deriving existence statements.
2. **Universal arithmetic soundness.** Prove the chosen exact integer/dyadic comparisons, directed rounding, addition/product/division/square-root witness checks, and complex-box operations preserve their stated closed-set semantics. For narrow reciprocals, prove the complete rectangle-extremum rule, including degenerate rectangles and origin exclusion. Prove finite graph composition by induction. A test suite is not a substitute for these proofs.
3. **Honest leaf boundary.** This first slice checks modular algebra and the fixed finite polynomial/arithmetic recipes, **not analytic exp/theta certificates**. Its generic semantic theorem preserves containment for real assignments satisfying the explicitly named seed, elementary-value and terminal-jet box hypotheses. Its closed acceptance theorem is about the concrete finite arithmetic checker, not a closed P bound. It must list those unresolved analytic leaf obligations explicitly. In particular arithmetic acceptance alone cannot label a Taylor interval as enclosing exp, a terminal finite sum as an actual theta jet, or a transported expression as the actual P value. The elementary and theta analytic soundness layers discharge those leaves later; no pending leaf becomes a new root assumption.
4. **Closed finite acceptance.** Obtain kernel-checked proofs that the selected concrete typed computations accept. Use ordinary trusted-kernel computation or explicitly checked witness proofs, with the generic soundness theorem applied afterward. Neither a Python Boolean nor an unchecked native evaluation supplies this step. Check the exact closed theorem types and transitive axioms; only the project's permitted standard axioms may remain. No `sorry`, added axiom, hidden certificate-correctness premise, or native-generated axiom is acceptable.
5. **Negative controls at the real acceptance boundary.** Reject altered target/type, a T sign or phase inconsistent with the fixed recipe, forward reference or cycle, reversed interval, denominator containing zero, negative root upper endpoint, missing required terminal-error allocation, falsely narrow arithmetic output or incorrect rounding witness. Tests must invoke the actual proposed checker, not only compare mutated JSON to a saved hash. A changed but mathematically valid graph for the same fixed recipe and claim may be accepted; byte identity is not the soundness criterion. Maintain separate conclusions for arithmetic rejection and analytic leaf obligations.
6. **Bounded resource evidence.** Before the implementation experiment, fix an explicit time/memory cap and fixture list. Record generation/serialization, elaboration, kernel checking, import/closure overhead, proof/data sizes and peak memory separately when that experiment is later authorized. Identify whether repeated reduction or literal/proof representation dominates. If a representative case fails the cap, stop and revise representation or chunking without weakening semantics. This memo runs no such experiment and predicts no throughput.

Completion of this milestone means the arithmetic checker and those concrete acceptance proofs are real Lean artifacts with audited types/axioms. It does **not** mean the spectral theorem is proved, the analytic wrappers have been supplied, or replay of all 929 nodes is cheap. Conversely, a generic soundness theorem without any closed acceptance proof does not complete the milestone.

## Scaling assumptions that remain unresolved

No Lean speed or memory estimate follows from Python runtime, the 929-node count, JSON byte size, or operation counters. Typed-data sharing may reduce repeated work; an eagerly unfolded shared definition may instead recompute it. Witnesses trade computation for certificate size and still require checking. Fixed-grid dyadics may avoid repeated rational normalization, but their Lean performance is unmeasured. The general compact signed-exponent checker remains a separate implementation need for huge-exponent records; do not expand those values into enormous rational denominators merely because the small pilot uses fractions.

Analytic parameters also change with height. Quadrature width a shrinks, the required grid and precision change, and high-height batching must preserve exact lattice, interpolation and normalization errors. Modular acceleration does not itself prove a production cost bound. No strict margin has been shown uniformly on the required family/height domains; equality on a desired weak-strip boundary can obstruct a stronger closed exclusion certificate.

The global rational-inverse prototype has quadratic-in-height structures under literal extrapolation.
