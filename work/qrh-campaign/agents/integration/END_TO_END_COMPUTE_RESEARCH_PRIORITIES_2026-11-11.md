## 2. How much can the half-size time transform remove?

Let F(N)=N log2(N)/2 and let sums include every actual character channel. The frozen new author packet packs the Hermitian polynomial into an N/2 complex transform. Its proposed base-grid cost is F(N/2)+J complex products, with J=ceil(25N/192). It preserves both inverse-character channels. Independent acceptance of that packet is separate from this comparison.

The saved ledger gives sum N=9,237,783,904,256 and sum J=1,202,846,693,883. Thus the exact prospective comparison is

    sum F(N/2) = F/2 - (sum N)/4 = 42,619,662,827,520;
    saved products = F - sum F(N/2) - sum J
                   = 46,035,708,085,765.

It also removes 92,071,416,171,530 complex additions and 138,107,105,780,187 complex rounds under that specified schedule. New conjugations, coordinate operations, packing roots and output accesses remain. Workspace can halve while the full real output array still has N entries. The 47,238,554,779,648 gross butterfly reduction must not be quoted as net products after dropping packing.

Use separate prices for generation, ordinary proof production and ordinary verification. For any one component write Cmul(P,Q), Cadd(W), Cround(...), Csetup and Ctraffic. The true saving is the sum of removed gate prices minus new gate/setup/traffic prices, evaluated at actual operand widths. If one substitutes average complex-product prices only, matching the entire retained large G bill requires

    small product price / large product price
      >= 1,004,103,686,197,888 / 46,035,708,085,765 > 21.8.

For an illustrative common three-real-product implementation, the large G plus residue-Horner product subtotal is 3G+2H=5,290,869,412,817,088. Matching that subtotal needs a small/large real-product price ratio greater than 38.3. These are sensitivity thresholds, not cost lower bounds; they omit additions, rounding, asymmetric factors, setup and all other categories.

A useful explicitly hypothetical schoolbook model is Cmul proportional to the product of operand bit widths. If both small factors are rho times the corresponding large factors, multiply the product saving fraction by rho^2. At rho=1, 3/2, 2, 3, its fraction of large G is respectively about 4.58%, 10.32%, 18.34%, 41.26%; against 3G+2H the corresponding fractions are about 2.61%, 5.87%, 10.44%, 23.49%. The exact fractions are in the receipt. These are scenarios, not measured width ratios or a universal multiplication law. Faster multiplication, a narrow twiddle operand, ordinary-PA lookup proofs or different rounding costs alter them.

Small raw time gates require 241--343 fractional bits, while raw output magnitude exponents range 54--69 in the successor ledger. Those output bounds are not the normalized butterfly's integer width, and fractional precision alone cannot price a product. The large circuit likewise has a proved per-circuit peak coefficient and asymmetric operand bounds. Consequently the half transform is useful and bounded; it is not justified to call it negligible, nor to call it the dominant end-to-end saving.

## 4. The next distinct large-category proof target

No inspected accepted packet removes the P20 residue-contraction category. Shifted jets reduce seed generation; primitive output pruning retains residue inputs. The earlier global-bottleneck report explicitly rejects a naive dense precomputed composite map as more expensive. The original source interpolation nevertheless supplies a precise new target: within one nearest-alpha cell, one fixed degree-19 polynomial is evaluated at alpha=a/c on an arithmetic grid.

Investigate an exact rational finite-difference circuit for that polynomial, in bounded contiguous numerator blocks. A possible representation clears the common c^19 denominator and the cell/normalization denominators, proves the finite-difference initial state, updates it by additions, and rounds only each consumed value back to the existing residue interface. This preserves the same finite polynomial; its original analytic tail and coefficient radii remain separate. It is not a new approximation or a spectral assumption.

Before investing in a broad proof, require this concrete feasibility gate:

1. Read the existing five-band conductor/height manifest. Count target units per exact cell/block, not all integer numerators as though they were old work. The final band has source D4096 but output D8192; the first four output D are512,1024,2048,4096. Fine anchors have denominator2D and |alpha-anchor|<=1/(4D). Fix endpoint ties explicitly.
2. Charge initialization, all nonunit steps needed to reach later units, exact denominator conversion, every addition and final output rounding. Short or sparse blocks can select the original Horner circuit. No occupancy distribution is assumed.
3. Prove actual integer sizes, including c^19 and cell offsets. Exact differences avoid accumulated numerical roundoff but can have much wider integers; this is not free. If rounded differences are proposed instead, prove their propagation over the chosen block length before comparing prices.
4. Compare the complete block cost with 19 Horner stages per old unit target at the same output error. Aggregate only blocks whose inequality is established. Require a material gain over the approximately 46 trillion small complex products above after width/setup charges, not merely fewer multiplication symbols.

The parent has now assigned the occupancy calculation separately. This note consumes no unfinished result from it. The proposed block theorem is the next proof target, not an adopted saving. Failure at the occupancy/width gate should stop this version. It need not trigger another unbounded parameter search.
