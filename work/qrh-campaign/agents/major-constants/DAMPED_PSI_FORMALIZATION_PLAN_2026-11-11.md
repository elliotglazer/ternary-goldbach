## 2. First reusable gap: finite Abel summation at real abscissae

The existing natural-index theorem cannot be applied by replacing a zero ordinate with its floor. That changes the weight. Sorting the roots and interpolating by their indices would add unnecessary machinery.

The following sufficient statement is genuinely generic. Let i range over a type with decidable equality, let S be a finite set, let `gamma,m : i -> Real`, and let `a,b : Real` with `a<=b`. Define

`N_S(t)=sum_{i in S, gamma(i)<=t} m(i)`.

Let `f,f' : Real -> Real`. Assume f is continuous on `[a,b]`, f' is continuous there, and `HasDerivAt f (f'(t)) t` for every t in `(a,b)`. Then

`sum_{i in S, a<gamma(i)<=b} m(i)*f(gamma(i))`

`= f(b)*N_S(b)-f(a)*N_S(a)-integral_a^b f'(t)*N_S(t) dt`. (A)

No nonnegativity of m, distinctness of ordinates, avoidance of endpoints, or ordering of S is needed for this identity. Multiple roots at one ordinate are simply different weighted atoms, or one atom with its full integer multiplicity.

Here is a complete proof suited to the inspected APIs. For one atom g, the product `f'(t)*1_{g<=t}` is measurable and integrable: it is a restriction of a continuous function on a compact interval. Compute its integral in three cases.

* If `g<=a`, it is `f(b)-f(a)` by FTC, including `g=a`.
* If `a<g<=b`, it is `f(b)-f(g)`. For `g<b`, obtain the right-ray integral by subtracting the left-ray indicator integral from the full integral. The predicates `g<=t` and `not(t<=g)` differ only at g. Use the volume-null singleton and `integral_congr_ae` before the subtraction, rather than claiming they are pointwise equal. For `g=b` the integral is zero, exactly as the formula states.
* If `b<g`, the integral is zero.

For each case, the identity

`f(b)*1_{g<=b}-f(a)*1_{g<=a}-integral_a^b f'(t)*1_{g<=t} dt = f(g)*1_{a<g<=b}`

is immediate. Multiply by m(i), sum over S, distribute finite sums, and invoke `integral_finsetSum`. This proves (A). In the degenerate case a=b all terms cancel and the interval integral and band sum vanish. Thus no exceptional endpoint convention is hidden in the proof.

The companion comparison lemma needs only these additional hypotheses: m is nonnegative on S; f is nonnegative on `[a,b]`; `f'<=0` there; `C,C' : Real -> Real` are continuous on `[a,b]` with the specified derivative relation in its interior; and `N_S(t)<=C(t)` for every t in `[a,b]`. With `n_a=N_S(a)`, (A), integral monotonicity, and integration by parts give

`sum_band m(i)*f(gamma(i)) <= f(a)*(C(a)-n_a)+integral_a^b f(t)*C'(t) dt`. (B)

Indeed retain `-f(a)n_a`, bound `f(b)N_S(b)` by `f(b)C(b)`, and bound `(-f')N_S` by `(-f')C`. The last two bounded terms equal `f(a)C(a)+integral f C'`. If an exact upper endpoint count `n_b=N_S(b)` is retained, subtract `f(b)*(C(b)-n_b)` from (B). This optional strengthening is not used by the selected 1500-head proof.

In the application S is a complete carrier through b; this makes `N_S(t)` the actual closed N(t) for every `t<=b`. A partial carrier must not be used to justify the exact lower endpoint `N(A)=1069`. The carrier completeness lemma therefore precedes (B).

## 3. Finite Gaussian tail lemmas remove an unnecessary limit

For each real `W>=1`, prove the bounded-interval statements

`integral_1^W exp(-v^2/2)/v dv <= exp(-1)` and

`integral_1^W exp(-v^2/2)*log(v)/v dv <= exp(-1/2)/2`. (C)

These suffice for the scalar proof. There is no need to establish a new improper-integral theorem merely because the paper bound was written with infinity.

For the first statement use `w=v^2`, with derivative `2v`. All reciprocal functions are continuous on the image `[1,W^2]`, as required by `integral_comp_mul_deriv'`. This gives half the integral of `exp(-w/2)/w`. Enlarge the finite upper endpoint to `S=max(W^2,2)` by nonnegativity. On `[1,2]`, compare `1/w` with `(3-w)/2`; on `[2,S]`, compare it with `1/2`. FTC evaluates the resulting two integrals to

`(1/4)*integral_1^2 (3-w)exp(-w/2) dw + (1/4)*integral_2^S exp(-w/2) dw`

`= exp(-1) - exp(-S/2)/2 <= exp(-1)`.

For the second statement use `log(v)<=v^2/2` on `[1,W]`, compare with `(v/2)exp(-v^2/2)`, and evaluate its integral as `(exp(-1/2)-exp(-W^2/2))/2`. This also proves the bound at W=1. The inequality for log follows, for example, by differentiating `v^2/2-log(v)` on `[1,W]`; its derivative `v-1/v` is nonnegative and its value at 1 is 1/2.

More generally fix `c>0`, `A>c`, `delta>0`, `delta*A<=1`, and any finite `V>=A`. Write `R=1/delta`, `Z=log(R/c)` and `ell=log(A/c)`. Nonnegativity permits replacing V by `V'=max(V,R)`. Split at R, drop the Gaussian on `[A,R]`, and substitute `v=delta*t` on `[R,V']`. Formula (C) then proves

`integral_A^V exp(-delta^2*t^2/2)*log(t/c)/t dt`

`<= (Z^2-ell^2)/2 + Z*exp(-1)+exp(-1/2)/2`. (D)

Every integral here is over a finite compact interval away from zero. Also `integral_A^V exp(-delta^2*t^2/2)/t^2 dt <=1/A` by comparison with `t^-2` and FTC. Applying (B) with `f(t)=exp(-delta^2*t^2/2)/t`, `C=G`, `c=2*pi`, and (D), then using `exp(-1)<.368` and `exp(-1/2)/2<.304`, gives precisely the frozen Dbar bound. This replaces the improper extension in the earlier presentation without changing its constant.

## 4. Weighted involutions and the actual zero carrier

The small algebraic helper can be stated with arbitrary `S : Finset i`, `J : i -> i`, `m,F : i -> Real`. Assume J maps S to S, `J(J(i))=i` for i in S, and `m(J(i))=m(i)`. Then

`sum_S m(i)*F(i) = (1/2)*sum_S m(i)*(F(i)+F(J(i)))`.

Apply `Finset.sum_nbij'` with both maps equal to J, then add the original sum to its reindexed copy. Positivity is unnecessary for the identity, but required when bounding each averaged term. There is no division by orbit size, so fixed points require no separate combinatorial case.

The substantial zeta-specific prerequisite is the complete carrier plus true-order transport. Follow the frozen symmetry packet: choose an auxiliary containing rectangle strictly taller than H, use all its zeros with `0<Im rho<=H`, and prove equivalence with actual nontrivial zeta zeros in this range. `zerosRectD_zetaF_strip`, `divB_eq_of_mem`, and `analyticOrderAt_xiF_eq` supply the source identities; completeness must also prove strict membership in the inner zero disk. This is why the auxiliary height exceeds H even though the band is closed at H.

For reflection, Mathlib `Analysis/Analytic/Order.lean:588`, `analyticOrderAt_comp_of_deriv_ne_zero`, applies to the holomorphic map `z -> 1-z`. It does not apply to complex conjugation. For conjugation the needed reusable result is: if an analytic function satisfies `F(conj z)=conj(F(z))` near the relevant points, then its finite analytic orders at z and conj(z) agree. Prove this by conjugating the local nonzero Taylor factorization: if `F(w)=(w-z)^m h(w)` near z and `h(z)!=0`, then near conj(z), `F(w)=(w-conj(z))^m conj(h(conj(w)))`; the last factor is analytic by its conjugated power series and remains nonzero. Combine with reflection to preserve order under `1-conj(z)`. No antiholomorphic derivative is asserted.

Exclude the real axis using the selected head condition and the existing `xiF_half_ne_zero`, before doubling positive ordinates by conjugation. The exact both-sign factor is then 2, with true multiplicities. Enlarge the nonnegative norm majorant of any explicit-formula prefix to the complete through-H carrier before applying the involution. A prefix need not itself be invariant.

## 5. Continuum derivative interface

Do not formalize the prose logarithmic-derivative argument by introducing log of every large expression unless useful. A smaller reusable helper is sufficient: on a closed real interval I, let positive differentiable functions u,v and functions a,b satisfy `u'<=a*u` and `v'<=b*v`. Product differentiation gives `(uv)'<=(a+b)uv`. If C>=0 and `v'<=b*v` with b>=0, then `(C+v)'<=b*(C+v)`. These are pointwise algebraic inequalities; continuity and `antitoneOn_of_hasDerivWithinAt_nonpos` convert a nonpositive resulting derivative into the endpoint bound.

Apply this to the actual frozen functions, with `L in [2233/100,35]`: `q=1/(500000*L^2*(L+10))`, `a0=1/(500L)`, `Z=log500+log L+log(L+10)/2-log(2*pi)`, and Dbar as in the scalar packet. The scalar lemma `Z<11` and the rational left-endpoint lower bound are separate inputs to this elementary calculation, not consequences of numeric sampling.

For `V(L)=cosh(h*(L+a0))`, use `Real.hasDerivAt_cosh` (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/DerivHyp.lean:352`), `cosh_pos` (`Analysis/Complex/Trigonometric.lean:848`), and the elementary exponential identity `0<=sinh(t)<=cosh(t)` for t>=0. This gives `0<=V'<=h*V`; no bound on tanh is needed. The bracket `H_A+V*Dbar` therefore has derivative at most `(h+3/L)` times itself. Combining it with `L*exp(-L/2)` and the decreasing positive factors yields coefficient `4/L+h-1/2<0`. The second bracket uses a constant cosh and gives `4/L-1<0`. All denominators and logs are positive on the closed interval; establish continuity there before applying the convex-set theorem. Boundary derivatives outside the interval are unnecessary.
