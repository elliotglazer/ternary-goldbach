## Necessary equal-trust interpretation

The proposed ordinary-checker experiment is a legitimate comparison of presently specified acceptance paths. It must not be presented as the full design space for efficient MM0. The MM0 specification makes the proof-file format implementation-dependent and defines the requirement in terms of derivability from the supplied axiom system. It does not require every conforming verifier to replay the present MMB instruction sequence. [Pinned MM0 specification](https://github.com/digama0/mm0/blob/0d414c0bfdaaeb7fea571895127abc1fa5a3d956/mm0.md).

A specialized arithmetic or bulk-bitset checker is therefore possible in principle. Its added acceptance rules must preserve derivability in the chosen theory, with exact input interpretation and statement binding. A constructive proof-translation contract is one route:

`Accept(spec, certificate) -> Prf_Gamma(expand(spec, certificate), target(spec))`.

The expansion can be justified without being materialized during accelerated acceptance. That is a changed checker or proof format, not an ordinary unchanged-MMB artifact. Proving only that an abstract checker is sound does not validate an arbitrary binary or an alleged execution; implementation, input and result correspondence still matter. Nor does this argument assert that PA proves its own global semantic soundness.

Equal treatment is important here. Lean's existing arithmetic implementation is inside its trusted checking path. It would be unfair to permit that implementation trust for Lean while requiring a fully machine-verified executable refinement for every analogous MM0 optimization. One can compare audited trusted implementations on both sides, or require stronger executable proofs on both sides. In either case, mathematical admissibility and exact theorem binding are required. Permitting new optimized checkers also permits Lean-side optimization; the prover name alone then determines even less about runtime.

This is a statement about the finite arithmetic and certificate computations under discussion. It is not a claim that all theorems provable in Lean's foundation are PA theorems, nor that MM0 fixes PA as its only possible axiom system. Encoding and theory-preservation obligations remain part of a meaningful comparison.

For the same reason, expanded MMB byte volume is not a universal lower bound on work or storage in every optimized architecture. Streaming, shared proof templates or a justified specialized acceptance path can change materialization costs. Ordinary proof steps still cost work when they are replayed. The reviewed note's proof-generation and storage columns are appropriate for the specific ordinary-MMB experiment, not mandatory costs of all efficient MM0 implementations.

## Runtime and workload verdict

No numeric Lean/MM0 runtime ratio follows from the checked sources. In particular, constant-time pointer comparison is not constant-time numerical verification, and native literal arithmetic does not make a whole reflective checker free. The older local binary-Goldbach experiments measured different operations and included explicitly schematic MM0 arithmetic assumptions; they cannot fill the missing matched checker timings for these subcell and interval certificates.

The proposed experiment uses relevant inputs. The local full-candidate and kernel-replay reports support the 125271 subcounts, 7004-prime base and 131072-bit pattern semantics. These are candidate data and a proposed checking contract, not completed Lean count proofs. The reciprocal corpus must retain its two known rejection outcomes, and the seed corpus must include the rational/expression binding and source soundness theorem. A Boolean result with no meaningful final theorem is insufficient on either side.

For an efficient-implementation comparison, freeze the mathematical workload while allowing each side reasonable representation, batching and caching choices. Do not require identical low-level loops or an artificially small word width. Report any excluded one-time proofs, parsing, specification checks, input certification and final assembly. Distinct batches help reveal setup costs, but three sizes do not by themselves justify extrapolation to unknown spectral certificate volumes.

The final accepted conclusion is consequently narrow and useful: the cited current-checker mechanisms differ, the note proposes a sensible first matched experiment, and there is no justified runtime ratio or fundamental one-system-only computational capability established here. The broader optimized-checker alternative remains unimplemented and unmeasured. No new proof status or production forecast follows from this review.
