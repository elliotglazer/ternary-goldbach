## Mark size and its uniform constant

`Descent/Marks.lean:15–103` defines a slot as the sum of its coefficients over primes of A in that slot, and a mark as the product over slots. Triangle inequality gives slot norm at most `card(list_i intersect A)`. Pairwise disjointness makes the sum of these intersection cardinalities at most card(A). Therefore

`|primeMark(A)| <= product_i card(list_i intersect A) <= 2^card(A)`.

Empty slots, an empty slot family and an empty A are included by the same formulas. There is no hidden factor depending on the number of slots. This would generally fail without disjointness; that hypothesis is used explicitly.

`Descent/MarkBounds.lean:11–39` identifies card(A) with the prime-support cardinality of the nonzero ideal generated by the prime product. The prime-ideal coprimality and nonzero generators justify that identification. It then applies `Arithmetic/SquarefreeDivisors.prime_support_subsets_bound` (line 83).

I checked that small-power argument itself at lines 83–126. For fixed e>0, choose N so every integer n>=N satisfies n^e>=2. The set of all ideals of norm at most N is finite. Splitting the prime support into this fixed small set and its complement gives a factor at most `2^(card small)` from small primes. Each large prime contributes `2<=Norm(P)^e`; their product ideal divides the original nonzero ideal, so its norm is at most the original norm. Thus the constant depends only on e and the fixed number ring. It is chosen before the pool, slot data, generators or A. This is a genuine subpower bound, not a large-pool asymptotic being applied uniformly without justification. The imported facts here are ideal norm finiteness, prime factorization/support divisibility and norm multiplicativity.
