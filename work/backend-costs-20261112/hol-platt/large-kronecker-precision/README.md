# Precision of the whole-axis convolution

The integer fold recovers the original precision for every one of the 125,400 large conductors. Replacing each eligible axis by exact convolution gives a candidate grid of 80 to 84 fractional bits. The original range was 80 to 105. Across 269,921,342,390 eligible slices, the summed capacity of one packed operand falls from 1.3242 PB to 1.1850 PB (10.51%). This is an arithmetic capacity calculation. It does not price runtime or traffic.

For one eligible even axis of length n, divide the Fourier sum by n for its error analysis. Each term has three chirp factors with unit true norm. Suppose the bound on the norm of an exact input center is M, and each stored chirp and rounding has complex error at most u = 2^(-p). Relative to the exact transform of those centers, the three chirp perturbations contribute at most M((1+u)^3−1). The prechirp rounding contributes at most u(1+u)^2. The final rounding contributes at most u/n. Exact integer convolution and denominator changes introduce no extra error. For n≥32 and p≥80 the sum is at most 4(M+1)u. This accounts for the new local arithmetic error. Existing input uncertainty propagates through the ideal normalized transform by the triangle inequality. Restoring the normalization before the next axis gives the corresponding unnormalized error.

The script substitutes coefficient 4 in the matched tensor error ledger. It retains the original short-axis coefficients, incoming regrid charge, stage magnitude tests, precision floor and final radius. It recreates the previous least accepted grid before examining each candidate. The previous coefficient on every eligible axis was at least 14. The new counts at p=80,81,82,83,84 are 29,447, 51,642, 40,011, 4,296 and 4. In 118,421 conductors the grid drops by at least one bit.

The calculation assumes that the actual gathered chart and projected labels implement the Fourier map, and that each generated chirp has the stated error.
