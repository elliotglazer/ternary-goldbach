# HOL4 import of the conductor-10,000 sign archive

Two successful HOL4 processes each read the same 339,562 signs four times per layout. The first process began with text, the second with binary. Each alternated the order across pairs. Each import constructed eight HOL lists and compared them with the existing CountReceipt definitions. Both paths decoded identical headers, sign runs and phase operands. The logs contain separate process CPU timings for decode, term construction and comparison. Times are reported to the nearest millisecond.

At 41,029,096,839,823 signs, multiply the sum of a single binary pass's phase times in seconds by 33,563.74 to obtain core-hours. The eight sums were 0.077, 0.091, 0.195, 0.089, 0.094, 0.134, 0.076 and 0.095 seconds. Their median gives **3,105 core-hours**, with an observed range of **2,551–6,545**. For planning a separately materialized archive, reserve about **6,550 core-hours** for this step at the measured occupancy. This upper observation has no statistical coverage guarantee. Decoding alone contributes about 34–67 core-hours. The common term builder has substantial timing variation.

The extrapolation assumes the same 58.4 signs per run, eight rows per 339,562 signs, shared checked definitions and warm filesystem reads. The 76,214-byte archive extrapolates to 9.21 decimal TB under that model. Larger coordinates, transfer, disk throughput, cold starts, producing scans, creating or exporting new definitions, CV input encoding and linking the analytic sign producer require their own estimates.

The joint transpose benchmark parses both text tables and constructs input encodings before timing the repeated evaluator. Its hot-minus-cold marginal price measures the evaluator on existing inputs. The loader here constructs character-major sign-run terms rather than the transpose benchmark's time-major input blocks. Charge this only for a separate archive ingestion requirement. When the producer supplies those sign terms inside the proof, charging for their reconstruction would duplicate work. The alpha-equivalence checks in this benchmark require preexisting checked definitions and do not certify a new numerical sign or provide a CV representation theorem.
