# Job-count diagnostic revision

`screen01` stopped with `unexpected job process` after about 0.11 seconds. Its receipt did not retain the queried count. There were no worker events. The recorded child exit code after job closure is not evidence that the numerical worker completed. This record cannot identify the cause of the count mismatch.

`run_screen_v3.py` preserves the numerical worker `worker_v2.py` unchanged. It retains the lifetime-count check and adds persisted observations before that check. It records raw accounting bytes and decoded fields, a bounded raw PID list, original handle/Popen PIDs, membership-query result, and child status both before and after the queries. It records the new job before launch, then the assigned worker before GO. A mismatch at that point stops before FLINT can be imported. The first three polls, every detected count/query anomaly, and final pre-close observations are fsynced to a new diagnostic file. At most 16 records and 64 KiB may be saved. This is one diagnostic attempt, not a retry scheme.

Microsoft documents `TotalProcesses` as a lifetime association count, including failed associations due to a job-limit violation. It separately documents transient `ActiveProcesses` increments in that case and reference-dependent decrements. Thus an observed active count need not equal currently executing workers; this does not justify assuming a mismatched lifetime count is harmless. See [the accounting structure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_basic_accounting_information). The [PID-list structure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_basic_process_id_list) identifies returned processes and also includes child jobs when nested.

The inspected campaign supervisor design already says to save raw accounting and PID observations before deciding, and rejects unexplained lifetime anomalies. V2 omitted that diagnostic preservation. V3 repairs the omission without reinterpreting the failed run or weakening the check.

The hard active-process limit, job membership and memory limits provide containment for the member process. Alone they do not prove that the job's lifetime involved only the intended process: denied spawn/assignment attempts can affect accounting, and a current snapshot does not reconstruct history. Direct base-interpreter launch and the PID-bound pre-import handshake further bind the actual evaluator. The next raw record is required before deciding whether any count policy should change. No transient-count explanation is claimed as the diagnosis.

After source review, the coordinator can invoke the same base-interpreter command as V2, replacing `run_screen_v2.py` by `run_screen_v3.py` and using a new run label. No run, import, syntax execution or FLINT evaluation was performed while preparing V3. V1, V2 and the failed run's files remain unchanged.
