# Batched exact Horner on consecutive numerators

At height index one, all 131,070 units of conductor 131071 and all 70,720 units of conductor 265800 produced exactly the same terminal-grid output under direct delayed Horner and integer generating-function batching. Every **preterminal** integer was also checked, including nonunits. The FLINT-backed series took 0.327 s versus 0.321 s for scalar Horner at the prime conductor and 0.251 s versus 0.183 s at the composite. The native crossover was not reached in these runs.

The coefficients are the 1,025 actual band-1 fine-anchor records in c10004-p20-source/h1-mixed.json. They depend on height and mesh, so they apply at the other conductors with their respective displacements. For c=131072 the experiment also used both edge-slab cells at heights 6337 and 6400. The structural manifest gives J=8795 at this conductor, making 6400 the actual last main height in band 1. These two tests used degree 19, 440-bit fields and 96 unit outputs apiece. Complete scalar times were 0.715 and 0.633 ms, versus 1.223 and 1.323 ms for the FLINT-backed series. These small wall measurements are illustrative.

## Integer identity and signed digits

Align the d+1 mixed-grid coefficients to grid94. For each real coordinate absorb the powers (2c)^(d−j) into these coefficients. The numerator P(i) evaluated at r0+1024i then lies on one common denominator (2c)^d across the cell. Here i indexes all L consecutive positive integers in the cell, including nonunits. Bound every |P(i)| using the maximum absolute value of the two endpoint displacements. Select a byte-aligned base B=2^w with the bound strictly below B/2.

Write the polynomial generating series as F(X)=Σ_{i≥0}P(i)X^i. Since P(i) has degree at most d, its (d+1)-st forward difference vanishes. Thus (1−X)^(d+1)F(X)=U(X) with degree(U)≤d. U_i=Σ_{t=0}^i (−1)^t binom(d+1,t) P(i−t) for i≤d. Evaluate d+1 direct scalar seeds. Store once per (L,d,w) the low L base-B coefficients of (1−X)^(-d−1), given by binom(i+d,d). The implementation requires those nonnegative binomials to fit the chosen field, checked by fixed-width encoding. The first L exact numerators are encoded by U(B) times this kernel modulo B^L.

Signed extraction adds (B/2)Σ_{i<L}B^i before reduction modulo B^L. Each resulting field stores P(i)+B/2 without carry, including for negative values. A positive integer unit a retains its two coordinates; divide each by (2c)^d with nearest rounding, ties upward. Proof of the bound on all digits and of the signed representation remains essential for a theorem-prover implementation.

The scalar comparison performs the same coefficient alignment and denominator preparation, then evaluates only units. Series timing charges cell geometry and width choice, all cache misses, d+1 seeds per coordinate, binomial differences, bigint work, extraction, unit filtering and all final divisions. Both implementations start with the mixed coefficients in memory. Raw head, upstream coefficient generation and formal checking lie outside both clocks. Additional independent checks compare direct power sums and high-precision Hurwitz enclosures at several sampled units per cell. The largest observed sampled norm error at low height stayed below 6.94×10^-29. All checked points met 633 times norm error below 2^-82.

## Certificate direction

A producer can supply the signed packed candidate S for the whole cell. Verify (1−B)^(d+1) S ≡ U(B) mod B^L. Since 1−B is odd, it is invertible modulo B^L. The pre-established digit bounds therefore determine every signed coefficient uniquely. The checker can use one short-by-long integer product or d+2 shifted binomial multiples. It need not compute the inverse kernel. To skip rebuilding the candidate from untrusted coordinates, the consumer must derive the actual per-residue outputs from the accepted packed integer with checked extraction and exact division.

cert.py generated its candidate independently by scalar Horner and separately recomputed the d+1 seeds for verification. At c131071, all 1,025 cells cost 46.4 ms for short-by-long FLINT congruences or 63.0 ms for shifted checks. Other measured wall costs were 42.7 ms setup, 39.2 ms seed/U calculation, 54.7 ms packing, 100.9 ms unit filtering/final division from the known candidate coordinate list, and 203.6 ms scalar candidate generation. Division from packed-only inputs also requires signed extraction and coordinate binding; the series generator times that combined operation. No HOL proof or acceptance speedup was measured.

For the +41 guarded band-1 main alternative, the certified degree histogram yields 44,739,119,112,240 complex samples, 723,261,050,398,992 retained complex stages, 1,446,522,100,797,984 scalar multiplies, the same number of scalar additions, and 89,478,238,224,480 terminal coordinate divisions. As a sensitivity model only, linearly interpolating between the prime degree-eight scalar timing of 2.451 μs/sample and the pooled degree-19 timing of 7.022 μs/sample over that degree histogram gives about 72,630 single-worker wall hours. Conductor widths, unit density and cell occupancy vary. The high-degree timing represents only 192 outputs. This proxy excludes source generation, proof construction, acceptance and output I/O.
