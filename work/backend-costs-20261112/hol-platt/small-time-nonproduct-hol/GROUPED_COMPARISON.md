The earlier 178,195–444,445 core-hour envelope priced one scalar centered split per one or two retained pieces, using a broad count of 918,246,327–1,836,492,654 calls and single-width endpoint extrapolations. It predates the enumerated diagonal schedule. It has no final coordinate decoder in that number.

The diagonal fold reuses the exact same tile04_bench.log and same full-sized mode-2-minus-mode-3 matched timing, with seed medians 0.723, 0.785 and 0.871 seconds. The geometry gives 1,051,852,215 products. Splitting each costs 219,982.52–222,467.78 core-hours using the width mix. Grouping the products before the split reduces the count to 163,046,382 (107,615,058 output tiles plus 55,431,324 preceding boundary diagonals), giving 34,201.25–34,730.61 core-hours. Only the 680-bit class is interpolated between neighboring widths. See ../small-time-diagonal/fold.py lines 96–115, fold.json, DiagonalScript.sml, and d05.log.

The internally generated three-tile scalar consumer is a separate measurement. It extracts coefficients, performs the three-real-convolution recombination and a sample-specific synthetic postchirp, rounds, counts positives and returns a Boolean. The 4,096-field price maps to 41.6k–60.0k hours across endpoint widths and does not establish a 65,536-field extrapolation or ordered sign-word production. The splitter and decoder are separate stages. A final sign-only consumer may keep packed coordinates inside an evaluator and emit certified ordered sign bits and the sample centers needed for interpolation repairs.
