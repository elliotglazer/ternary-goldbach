# From grouped Bluestein output to sparse heads

The full-height conductor-10,000 pilot uses the archived four Gaussian spectra (256-bit grid), n=524288, J=68267, and the 240-bit fixed-point time transform. actual.py reconstructs the complex prechirps and conjugate kernel, multiplies the signed tiles with a 544-bit field radix, sums products on each diagonal, and performs one centered split per grouped diagonal. For every channel the observed retained interval straddles physical output tile indices 2 and 3. FLINT Arb at 320 bits requires a unique nearest integer for each cosine and sine coordinate of exp(pi i k²/n) on the 240-bit grid. The convolution uses exact integers. No saved raw grid value is used to form a convolution coefficient.

For the three real convolution coefficients A, B and C, the complex integer is (A-B, C-A-B). For a 240-bit postchirp (u,v), the normalized real coordinate is the nearest integer to ((A-B)u-(C-A-B)v)/2^499, including the transform normalization by 2^19. Restoring the unnormalized pilot scale at 220 bits divides that integer by two. The head is the floor on the retained row's grid, biased into its unsigned storage width. Physical field i corresponds to saved sample i-2J and to chirp index i-2J-128. Contiguous sparse rows can therefore cross physical-diagonal boundaries.

All four emitted head files are byte-identical to the existing sparse-head inputs. Across the 71,325 selected samples the restored raw integer differed from the archived independently enclosed FFT grid integer by at most one 2^-220 unit. There are 9,004 distinct positions needed for 103 retained interpolation stencils and 62,321 other sign-witness positions (counts sum over channels). The per-row width and exponent match the existing packet, preserving all existing enclosure and interpolation checks. Eight negative controls perturb an actual grouped field, once at a repair support and once at a sign-only slot on each channel. ## Short input-field certificate

top_fields.py reads just the leading bytes of each selected centered coefficient, with the discarded bits treated as an interval. For the postchirp numerator it uses A(u+v)+B(v-u)-Cv and takes all endpoint products of the three signed intervals before rounding. With 20 guard bits for the coefficients and 128 retained fractional bits per chirp coordinate, both endpoints round to the exact same final head at all 71,325 selected points. At 96 chirp bits, 2,123 ambiguous cases require full arithmetic. At 56 chirp bits, 35,868 require full arithmetic. Native fallback computes the full integer value only for an ambiguous interval. These are observations on the actual conductor-10,000 four-channel spectra; other inputs can use the same exact fallback.

check_arb_heads.py repeated four full 524,288-point interval DFTs of the pinned Gaussian coefficients at 336-bit precision. For every saved sample it proved directly in Arb that the DFT coordinate lies within one head unit of its emitted center. The worst ratio was 0.9999970777378284, across all sign-only sites and interpolation supports. The remaining analytic theorem connecting the Gaussian spectrum to the Hardy functions is separate.
## Which split work can be shared

Let H be the radix for one complete 65,536-field tile, R the single-field radix and C the integer formed by repeating R/2 in all fields. The ordinarily centered diagonal sum z has low signed polynomial L and carry Q with z=HQ+L. The valid coefficient bounds give 0<=L+C<H. Consequently divmod(z+C,H) returns Q and L+C together. The upper diagonal carry is unchanged. Bias conversion for those low coefficients needs no second whole-tile split. Extracting individual selected lanes within the low tile, applying their chirps, rounding and serializing the sparse heads remain consumer work.

The integrated genuine native benchmark starts from three actual double-width z values per physical tile. It checks every high carry. The baseline splits and checks all three ordinary signed lows. The ordinary consumer first takes that same split, biases the three low results, then emits the selected heads. The fused consumer takes the bias-adjusted split, uses its remainders directly, and emits identical compact heads. The median paired incremental cost above the already-paid split was 0.864 seconds ordinary and 0.707 seconds fused over all eight physical tiles, in five alternating-order repetitions. Median paired fusion credit was 0.166 seconds. These rates include native verification checks; they are distinct from stock HOL proof costs. The full native records are in native-integrated.csv and integrated-summary.json.
