# Output counts and remaining CPU allowance at guard +640

The small-conductor schedule has 7,499 conductors, 18,477,108 primitive character lanes and 1,787,257,220,237 retained coarse samples. For each row of the +640 schedule, multiply the primitive-character count by one plus the final included height index. The conductor-10,000 row, for example, ends at index 84,864. This sum reproduces the upper live-sample count in [consumer_scenario.json](consumer_scenario.json).

The large-conductor transform schedule has 4,333,246,843 primitive character lanes and 39,241,839,619,586 outputs. Together the families have 4,351,723,951 lanes and 41,029,096,839,823 outputs. The small group contributes 4.3561% of the outputs. Its average lane has 96,728 samples, compared with 9,056 for the large group. These counts exclude additional repair samples. The fused-sign scenario including the measured transpose-and-scan replacement costs 783,638–795,534 core-hours. Under an 800,000-core-hour cap, this leaves 4,466–16,362 hours. Spreading the remainder uniformly over both samples and families would allow 0.392–1.436 microseconds per sample. Spreading it by character instead gives 3.694–13.535 milliseconds per lane. These are alternative allowances for the same remaining work. Neither measures its actual cost. [Joint timing cross-check](transpose_scan_crosscheck.py)

The alternate small-family raw projection has a measured model increment of 16,669–17,109 core-hours over direct head-packet handoff. Subtracting the corresponding pair_h endpoint from inner_h before adding the difference to the revised fused scenario gives 800,308–812,643 core-hours. This scenario still excludes any separate archive-write, reload or proof-transport charge. The study measured the projection and reported no incremental filesystem I/O rate.
