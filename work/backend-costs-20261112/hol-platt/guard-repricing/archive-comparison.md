# Producing a sign archive on the +640 schedule

The text packer reproduced the HOL4 pilot's `packets.txt` byte for byte on each of five local runs. It read 4,136,360 bytes containing 339,562 strict signs at conductor 10,000, packed 5,814 runs, and wrote 181,872 bytes. The five CPU times on this replay were 0.421875, 0.406250, 0.375000, 0.390625 and 0.359375 seconds. Two other five-run batches gave medians of 0.421875 and 0.453125 seconds. The timer starts with sign files already available and includes the original packer's decode, count and consistency checks. [Packer and reproduction](../grid-sign-join/benchmark_run_archive.py)

The two families schedule 41,029,096,839,823 coarse outputs at guard +640. Transferring the three pilot median rates to one retained strict sign per scheduled output costs 13,111–15,209 core-hours. The calculation is `outputs × median CPU seconds / (339562 × 3600)`. It assumes the pilot's parsing cost and similar occupation of its 60-sign records. Later repairs add positions. The calculation applies to a separately executed producer when it writes an intermediate archive. An implementation that hands checked sign packets directly to the count must charge only the work that it actually repeats. [Scheduled counts](family_headroom.md)

The 12.80-second historical HOL import, scan and count export covers overlapping work. None of the rows adds that entire duration a second time. Transferring the pilot’s bytes per sign would yield 22.0 TB of text packets or 9.2 TB with the measured binary record layout. Those are the sizes of one copy at the pilot density. They do not include inputs, replicas, possible wider offsets, other proof data or repair archives. [Transpose calculation](transpose_scan_crosscheck.py) · [Binary benchmark](../grid-sign-join/binary-archive-results.json)

In a separate HOL4 test, two fresh processes reconstructed the binary sign-run terms and compared them to the eight previously checked lists. At the median cost this would add 3,105 hours for an explicit archive reread. The slowest measured pass would add 6,545 hours. These operations require preexisting definitions. The measurements omit building new CV input proofs from those terms, transporting definitions, and creating the binary archive from scratch. The text-packer and binary-loader measurements involve different file formats, so combining them requires a separately costed conversion or a direct binary producer. [Kernel loader and pricing](../archive-loader/pricing.md)

Helfgott estimated about 160,000 historical core-hours for odd conductors through 150,000 and even conductors through 300,000. The present schedule uses 132,900 and 265,800 respectively and has its own heights. Platt's wider run used about 400,000 hours. The nonprincipal row with separate packing is 4.85–4.93 times the first reference and 1.94–1.97 times the second. Neither comparison measures a slowdown on identical hardware and inputs. To come within a factor of ten of 160,000, this route has about 811,000–824,000 hours for the remaining work. That is about 71–72 microseconds per scheduled output if all of it were spent uniformly there. A first checked end-to-end total still needs source enclosures, fallback signs and repairs, proof transport, and successful Turing counts across the domain.
