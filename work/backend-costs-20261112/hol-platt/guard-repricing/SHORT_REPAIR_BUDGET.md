# Short interpolation for the nonprincipal zero count

Eight full-height conductor tests produced 270 interior repair requests in 936,788 coarse intervals. Twelve nearby coarse amplitudes recovered every archived reference sign. Eight recovered 260. The inputs had 16 magnitude bits and a sign per sample with a shared binary exponent within each stencil. The weights had 24 fractional bits. Each sign comparison allowed for sample rounding, coefficient rounding, and the analytic interpolation remainder. The inputs in these tests came from Gaussian grids converted to the completed Hardy normalization. The analytic comparison and the strip bound still require HOL4 proofs. [Per-conductor results](strip-repair-panel-stress.json) · [Packets](strip-repair-packets-stress.tsv) · [Strip bound](../dispatched-score/LARGE_HARDY_DERIVATIVE_AUDIT.md)

The strip estimate admits a single integer majorant for a whole conductor, parity, and strip width. Write the integer right boundary as $b$, the parity as $a$, the half-width as $B=b-1/2$, and $p=(b+a-1)/2$. For the retained nonnegative height range ending at $V$, use

$$
M=6\,2^{\lceil 9pB/(4(b+2))\rceil}
\left\lceil\sqrt{(q/3)^{b+a}(b+2+V)^{b+a-1}}\right\rceil .
$$

This follows from the strip estimate with disk radius $B$, using $2\sqrt{2\pi}<6$, $\pi>3$, $\sqrt2<3/2$, and $\log 2>2/3$. At a repair position $x$, the error of an $m$-node interpolant is at most $M\prod_i|x-t_i|/B^m$, in physical height units. For fixed nodes, the exact rational product and the rounded coefficients depend only on the offset within the stencil. The 270 packets use 40 coefficient tables and 14 selected conductor/parity/strip majorants. A separate integer check validates the rounding of every coefficient and every remainder ceiling. Perturbing a coefficient by two units is rejected for every table. The stored sample bit width and the input accuracy required from the spectral calculation are different quantities. On these 270 repairs, all signs survive extra uncertainty of $2^{-9}(|v_i|+1)$ at each node $i$, in units of the sample quantum with integer center $v_i$. At $2^{-8}$, 268 survive. If the extra uncertainty is instead the same at every node and proportional to the largest magnitude in the stencil, 13 bits suffice for every repair and 8 suffice for 143. These are tolerance tests on high-accuracy source samples. The production transform needs a measured path through its own radii, especially at its low precisions. ## Higher-precision retries

The generic rounding implication has now passed the stock HOL4 kernel. It permits a different input radius at each node, with both sign conclusions. The timed large transform emits signs and caches unit gamma phases. Its interpolant needs a separate positive magnitude factor to recover the completed Hardy amplitudes. The time-dependent part is shared across all conductors. The maximum large-family index is 84,880, so both parities together need only 169,762 entries. The 24-bit magnitude table occupies 679,048 bytes. Native interval generation took 2.20 CPU-seconds. Independent direct-gamma and reflection-identity checks took 4.30 CPU-seconds. Its interpretation as the analytic gamma magnitude still requires a formal proof. [HOL4 rounding theorem](../guard-compact/CompactRadiiScript.sml) · [Export](../guard-compact/radii.log) · [Table receipt](gamma-magnitude-table.json)

Recomputing an entire conductor and height for one difficult character can dominate the cost. As a sensitivity test, suppose repair starts occur independently at the observed pooled rate. Suppose a fraction $f$ of repairs require a higher-precision repeat at all twelve source heights. For $n$ characters, an interior height has probability $1-(1-f\lambda)^{12n}$ of sharing at least one affected stencil. Weighting each such height by its actual number of characters gives 15.5% of large-family outputs touched when $f=0.1\%$, and 71.3% when $f=1\%$. The union bounds with the same assumed marginal rates are 17.5% and 86.1%. These quantities measure affected outputs under the hypothetical policy. A producer that retains its source values and projects only the requested characters can have a different cost. [Full sensitivity calculation](repair-budget.json)

Platt reports that about three functions per million still needed special treatment after successive upsampling stages through a factor of 512. His exceptional cases included indeterminate central values, missed double or triple crossings, and auxiliary Turing windows with missing zeros. That historical rate describes the final tail of his own algorithm. Applying it as the first-retry rate for the short-stencil producer would skip the work of reaching that point.
