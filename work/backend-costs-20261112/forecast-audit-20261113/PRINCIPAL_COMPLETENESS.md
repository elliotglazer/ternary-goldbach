## 2. What a principal receipt must still prove

A sufficient RH-only design chooses a rational top Tstar>=10^8 and proves:

1. A lower count K of distinct critical-line multiplicity units at positive ordinates through Tstar, with exact coordinates, disjointness and actual continuous real Hardy normalization.
2. An upper bound N_+(Tstar)<=U with the actual principal pole/argument normalization, and strict U<K+2.
3. The separate real-axis exclusion and conjugation/order transport needed to cover the full closed carrier.

An off-line positive zero and its distinct reflection 1-conj(rho) would contribute at least two units outside the critical lower count. This proves RH, but not N_+=K, simplicity, a complete ordinate list or a reciprocal-sum identity. The stronger test U<K+1 gives exact saturation; it is an alternative if such an output is needed.

The upper bound can come from a completely certified zero-free horizontal top path with the principal argument formula, or a principal integrated Turing certificate with its own auxiliary evidence and allowance. The nonprincipal constant/formula cannot be substituted. Top-path nonvanishing concerns the entire path, not one endpoint sample. The closed-count Turing route can handle boundary zeros through its actual a.e./limit proof. Trying a top in (10^8,10^8+1] is a possible bounded search policy, not a guaranteed successful certificate.

Shards can prove only lower counts with exact seam/disjointness checks, followed by one global upper-count check. A local exact count at every shard boundary is optional discovery/debugging work. Historical ordinate archives can guide sample selection without entering the trusted mathematical receipt, but every retained coordinate/sign/count and its source identity still needs checking. Archive indices, native interval output or a checksum are not replacement proofs.

No exact actual N(10^8) has been established by this audit. The estimate's 250,000,001 sample sites are an illustrative schedule, not an authenticated zero count. Its unshared Riemann-Siegel main-sum ceiling of 4095 terms at t<=100000001 gives 1,023,750,004,095 scheduled summands. This is neither a multiplication count nor a lower bound on a shared evaluator. It does not include sufficient sign margins, correction/tail improvements, phase certification or count closure. The fixed analytic K0 error cannot be reduced just by increasing arithmetic precision.

## 3. Nonprincipal sign evidence versus full zero-count coverage

For a primitive nonprincipal character, let C_chi(T) count **all** actual zeros in 0<Re(s)<1, |Im(s)|<=T, including real zeros and true multiplicity. Let K be a proved lower count supported on the critical line. With H=T+h, h>0, the reviewed integrated-count interface is

\[
C_\chi(T)\le U=\frac{\overline A-W+2b}{h},\qquad
h(K+2)-\overline A+W-2b>0.
\]

Here Abar is an upper enclosure of the integrated canonical Gamma/main term, b bounds each of the two canonical S integrals, and W sums H-v for disjoint certified auxiliary brackets (u,v) inside (T,H). These must be actual right endpoints, not estimated root locations. Same-character reflection excludes an off-line pair, including pairs on the real axis or closed top boundary. As above, the <2 rule proves finite RH only; <1 is the stronger exact-count choice.

For nonreal characters the positive chi and inverse-chi channels supply both ordinate signs. For real characters the positive contribution is doubled. One count closure per inverse orbit can be shared, but positive inverse-channel data and the two S allowances cannot then be halved again. Exact label enumeration, primitivity, inversion, parity, root-phase convention and equality of requested heights remain binding obligations. If n_real is the actual number of self-inverse primitive labels, the orbit count is (n_labels+n_real)/2; this audit does not invent n_real from an asymptotic ratio.

A sign sample alone proves no zero. Two opposite strict signs of the same proved real continuous function give a critical zero in their interval. Disjoint certified intervals give K. They do not prove the absence of unobserved off-line or even-multiplicity zeros. An uncertain imaginary part enclosing zero does not prove the function is real. A sign-preserving elementary phase proxy is also not automatically the canonical Gamma phase needed in Abar.

Central K0=0 is sound in the closed <2 interface; a separate central nonvanishing pass is not intrinsically mandatory. But omitted central or boundary multiplicity consumes the available deficit, so a failed comparison may require additional evidence. Strict sign tests cannot certify an exact zero sample, and ordinary sign changes alone may miss even multiplicity. Repositioning, a stronger evaluator, local multiplicity evidence or another complete counting method is needed when applicable. Increasing p only decreases the terms that actually depend on p. Fixed source, phase and interpolation allowances remain. An unresolved orbit remains unresolved; neither true RH nor an assumed typical spacing proves this receipt terminates.

The reviewed finite-Blaschke alternative gives b<10+(9/8)log(cH/(2pi)), hence b<30.25 when the logarithm is below 18. This is a paper-level analytic input requiring implementation, not a measured closure cost. The older larger allowance and alternative published Turing bounds are other choices; do not add their allowances or run all their producers as mandatory work. Even with b=30.25, h=50 closes only if the actual deficit D=(Abar-W)/h-K is strictly below .79. A short auxiliary schedule alone never proves this condition.

## 5. Native historical calibration: useful, but a different acceptance boundary

Platt's 2013 Dirichlet computation reports approximately 400,000 native core-hours for 29,565,923,837 primitive L-functions up to modulus 400,000, with roughly 3.8e13 zeros excluding Turing auxiliaries. It used a different height profile and algorithms shared across characters/heights. Its tiny remaining hard-function fraction was reported after routine upsampling, not at the raw 5/64 grid. Repairs included higher precision, hidden pairs/triples and changes to the Turing region. These figures support including discovery and count repairs; they do not justify multiplying current label counts by a universal per-label rate or assigning that historical failure rate to the new evaluator. [Platt, §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3087).

Platt–Trudgian report rigorous interval verification through 3,000,175,332,800, covering 12,363,153,437,138 zeros, using about 7.5 million core-hours on 3.6 GHz Xeons. They used sign changes with Turing completeness and avoided unnecessary high-precision ordinate isolation. Parameters were retuned for the much higher range. This is useful native algorithm evidence, not an ordinary-kernel certificate timing. Linear rescaling to 10^8 is not established, and the larger theorem is not part of the selected principal bill. [Platt–Trudgian, §§1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09765).

Both computations combine numerical generation and their native rigorous validation; neither measures source-to-Lean/MM0/HOL theorem production, encoded-object binding, analytic-library construction or final TG assembly. Their zero counts must not be substituted for the current metadata's candidate samples.

## 6. What the end-to-end estimate must still price

The current backend report explicitly measures bounded arithmetic/finite fixtures. Its butterfly theorem checks rounded integer arithmetic, ranges and list shapes; it does not certify root enclosures, actual L inputs, whole-transform analytic error or zero counts. Its complete finite Goldbach interval is genuine finite evidence, but supplies no spectral throughput. Backend setup, input conversion, acceptance, export and independent replay also have different measured boundaries. None of these timings removes the following rows.

| Cost block | Already counted sharing to preserve | Unmeasured obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Principal generation | One chosen RH producer through 10^8, shared head if actually used | Evaluator selection, truncation/phase/error proofs, sample discovery, failed attempts, exceptions and real-axis treatment |
| Principal acceptance | Lower-count shards and one global upper-count certificate are sufficient | Actual signs, disjointness, principal count normalization, closed endpoint proof, coordinate/source binding and formal final assembly |
| Nonprincipal sample acceptance | Shared seeds/transforms and inverse positive channels; setup once per valid lifetime | Actual source/phase certificates, complete error transport, strict sign margins, retained query arithmetic and proof/data transport |
| Nonprincipal completeness | Abar and analytic allowances can share conductor/parity data; closure per inverse orbit | K/W extraction, exact ordering and seams, canonical integral enclosures, strict surplus, unresolved centers/multiplicities and adaptive extension |
| Formal infrastructure | Shared arithmetic and analytic libraries once in the total | Source-faithful finite-RH wrappers, ordinary checker soundness, cache/import identities, final standard-foundation TG join |

For forecasting, separate native discovery (including failed repairs), certificate construction/first acceptance, export/I/O, and optional independent replay. A verifier can omit failed exploratory DAGs, but the generator's time cannot be erased. Replacement benchmark rates must replace corresponding modeled arithmetic slots, not be multiplied into all these other blocks indiscriminately.

Principal closure is inside the principal allowance; nonprincipal closure is a separate row. Already priced auxiliary/guard transforms, phase setup and retained query arithmetic must be deducted from any more detailed replacement allowance for the same work. Finite binary/ladder costs are separately added once; alternate enlarged-principal gap routes replace parts of that partition rather than supplement it automatically.

The highest-value missing calibration is a complete source-to-sign-to-count block: actual principal lower witnesses plus a valid upper-count certificate, and a nonprincipal inverse orbit with actual K, W, Abar and strict surplus. Record attempted and retained samples, analytic versus arithmetic error, failed work, native discovery, proof construction/first acceptance, export, optional replay and peak memory. A single easy sign or a single successful block would still not establish the family-wide repair distribution. No such new experiment is performed or authorized by this audit.
