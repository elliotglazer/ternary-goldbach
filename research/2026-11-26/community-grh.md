# Choosing and compiling the GRH proof for ternary Goldbach

On 26 November 2026, stock Lean 4.35.0 on Linux accepted the compact Dirichlet GRH proof in the Goldbach project. The final run compiled 13 modules in 138.063 seconds using 781 previously compiled modules. The project then combined the resulting RH and GRH theorems with the finite and analytic Goldbach proofs. A fresh Lean process imported the all-input theorem and reported precisely `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. This chapter traces how the project chose the GRH source and what those two minutes measured. [Linux evidence and exact output](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/tree/c6368decde84d4be04943117a2bfef991f9b1831/verification/community-grh/compact/joined-theorem-3ca2fcf7)

## The original question, 10–22 November

The initial target was Charlie Reams’s `cde-moebius` branch at `23b2d68c`. Its all-input theorem took two explicit mathematical inputs, `PlattGRH` and `BinaryGoldbachInput`. The former encodes finite zero-location information for Dirichlet L-functions. The latter asserts binary Goldbach through $4\times10^{18}$. The project’s first question was whether OpenAI’s quasi-Riemann hypothesis (QRH) proof could discharge the zero-location input. The relevant source theorem excluded zeros strictly to the right of $7/8$ for all positive moduli, with the exception for the principal character at its pole. The accompanying zeta theorem gave the corresponding nonvanishing statement. Our 10 November source audit identified the exact declarations and toolchain difference. It left a kernel replay of that QRH dependency open. [Original Goldbach root](https://github.com/calr3/ternary-goldbach/blob/23b2d68ca344203d4384e0aff7affa4ec034bef9/TernaryGoldbach/MainTheorems.lean) · [QRH source and integration audit](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/outputs/qrh-blueprint-104/chapters/01-provider.md)

A zero with real part $3/4$ illustrates the issue. QRH permits it, whereas a bound placing a low zero on the critical line would exclude it. In an explicit formula the size of a zero’s contribution depends on $x^{\Re\rho}$, so a proof that only places the zero at or left of $7/8$ changes the numerical error budget. One developed alternative required a finite-family bound $\Re\rho\le111/200=0.555$ through specified heights and a global $7/8$ strip beyond. The archived calculation targeted major-arc lower coefficient 1.0404 and minor-arc upper coefficient 1.0376, with a final positive reserve of 0.00009 after the other allowances. The finite spectral certificate, analytic bridges and part of a finite prime-error estimate remained obligations. A reader can follow the exact inputs and thresholds in the reviewed blueprint. [QRH closure audit](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/outputs/qrh-blueprint-104/chapters/100-selected-closure-audit.md)

Two alternatives explain why the campaign changed direction. Certifying Platt-style data directly would retain the original analytic estimates and require a kernel-checkable account of the finite zero computation. The HOL4 experiments established individual kernels and timings. Separately, a route through Zinoviev’s GRH-conditional $10^{20}$ threshold promised a smaller interval for ternary Goldbach. Reviewing its totient inequality exposed exceptions, including one at 3,990. The analysis supplied a restricted corrected inequality and proposed repairs for the remaining range. At the November 22 handoff the downstream arc bounds were open. Both routes remain documented with their respective open assumptions and checked examples. [Historical and HOL4 handoff](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/historical.md) · [Zinoviev calculation and its scope](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/zinoviev.md)

Levent Alpöge’s release supplied the stronger theorem directly. The community preserved the original Lean 4.33.1 sources at `40e56bab`. The practical obstacle was combining that version with Goldbach’s Lean and Mathlib definitions. On November 22 the backport had compiled analytic modules including `XiPartial` and `WeilLines`. A later ARM64 Lean 4.33.1 receipt recorded the all-input theorem with three axioms using the released compiled GRH proof. The receipt expressly records `rebuilt_release_from_source: false`. Its 459.76 CPU seconds measure the last specialization and write using those existing objects. That earlier result explains the motivation for a single project that anyone could build from the GRH sources. [Original community archive](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/tree/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005) · [November 22 backport handoff](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/large-case.md) · [Lean 4.33.1 result receipt](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/proof/evidence/CompletedGoldbach-receipt.json)

## Which source was “the community GRH proof”?

On 25 November the answer changed within hours. At the first community pin, `756a7025`, the project was still developing the earlier Alpöge source family on Lean 4.37.0-rc1, with Mathlib `31ce06ea`. At the later community pin, `686902ae`, the repository root contained the readable edition of Sernau’s rewrite on Lean 4.35.0 and Mathlib `56a00585`. The README identifies the former development branch as `alpoge` and points to `grh-lean` for the release containing both Sernau editions. The Goldbach build selected the compact edition at the immutable Sernau/community release `72fead10`. Its toolchain and Mathlib matched the original Reams checkout. [Earlier community source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/tree/756a70250dcba9162b99d4ea35c186558434c17e) · [Community explanation of the switch](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/686902ae1da717a63faf52d2dc6a50e5722942cd/README.md) · [Two-edition release](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/tree/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207)

## What the time estimates covered

- **Earlier community 4.37 source.** Its maintainers described a full build as taking hours. Our first source replay exited at the challenge-build stage. That run supplied no completed-build time for the 58,947-file source tree. The community also offered already-built project artifacts to shorten editing setup. [4.37 build instructions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/756a70250dcba9162b99d4ea35c186558434c17e/README.md)
- **Readable and compact 4.35 sources.** Each edition’s README gives a budget of one to two hours on 12 cores with 24 GB of memory and roughly 85 additional minutes if Mathlib must build from source. Multiplying the quoted wall time by 12 yields **12–24 allocated core-hours**, or about 17 additional allocated core-hours for the quoted Mathlib build. Actual user-plus-system CPU hours require a process or cgroup measurement. The README gives the same interval for both editions. It cannot establish that the compact edition compiles four times faster just because it has 4.23 times fewer physical lines. [Readable build instructions](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/README.md) · [Compact build instructions](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh-compact/README.md)
- **Independent checking.** The authors separately reported a Comparator run with a stand-in for the sandbox and an unlimited stack, advising several hours and 64 GB. A local GRH build, an export and replay of the GRH theorem, and a protected export and replay of the complete Goldbach theorem cover different work. The two-hour upstream estimate alone gives no measurement for either the finite Goldbach certificate or Palomar’s two mandatory checkers. [Upstream checks](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh-compact/README.md) · [Palomar verification steps](https://github.com/PalomarRegistry/PalomarSubmission/blob/046554302064b2c83c2b924bea6db87fc5900c68/README.md)

## Converting 794 files into an importable package

The initial packaging commit `3fc4113f` put the Apache-licensed compact source in `CompactGRH/src`, added Lean module headers and public imports, and retained the attribution and source hashes. A comparison with the successful candidate shows further changes in **82 of the 794 source paths**. This count compares the two integration commits after the initial conversion. We also compared the declarations and proof bodies at the top of the proof. The declarations in `New.Endgame.LongForms`, `New.Endgame.ShortAndEnough`, `New.Main`, `Main`, and the Mathlib-only `Statements` file match the compact upstream text after the converted headers. [Initial import](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/commit/3fc4113f8ec45fc13845166cf27c5869a03a012d) · [Conversion versus successful candidate](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/compare/3fc4113f8ec45fc13845166cf27c5869a03a012d...3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b) · [File-level provenance](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/CompactGRH/source-manifest.json)

Three examples illustrate why an edit to an import header can require a proof repair several modules downstream.

1. **Visibility of definitions.** `HolomorphicIntegral` needed the implementation of a p-adic measure operation during elaboration. A private `import all Mathlib.NumberTheory.Padics.Measure.Basic` made that implementation available locally. The original public import continued supplying its public interface to downstream files. [Tested import repair](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/commit/fc729595d20448d3689f706378879a30a323a762)
2. **Default proofs at an importing declaration.** In `ValuationPair` and `ContinuousCocycles` a proof-valued default on a section variable could fail once a consumer requested the generated default. For the cocycle maps the producing module could emit a warning, then `ArtinDefs` or `DiscreteUnits` failed while elaborating a use of the map. Moving the same default hypothesis to the public declaration bound it in the right scope. Checking the resulting compiled declaration’s axioms caught an occurrence of `sorryAx` in a control where a compiler exit code alone would have concealed it. [ValuationPair source](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/CompactGRH/src/Lib/General/Groups/ValuationPair.lean) · [Cocycle maps](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/CompactGRH/src/Lib/ClassField/GaloisCohomology/ContinuousCocycles.lean) · [Native compilation and axiom evidence](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/c6368decde84d4be04943117a2bfef991f9b1831/verification/community-grh/compact/native-source-repairs-b5b23824.json)
3. **An argument whose value Lean could no longer infer.** In `PoincareSeries` the first error occurred while elaborating `U.conj _`. The remaining goal required the unitary-matrix identity for a specific matrix. Giving Lean the existing argument `V.xFin` closed the goal and also resolved the downstream errors mentioning `finiteEquiv`. In the last failing file, `New.Rows.Separation`, the repair made the splitting character, the exponent, and the tame component explicit in a signed pair. The theorem statements survived both changes. [Poincaré series source](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/CompactGRH/src/New/Filling/PoincareSeries.lean) · [Final signed-pair repair](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/commit/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b)

## Linux compilation rounds

The Linux worker retained outputs from earlier runs. It admitted a module from a previous run only when the source and its import ancestry passed the reuse checks. Thus a failed build could still contribute separately completed modules. This made the feedback loop cheap enough to follow the first real errors through the proof. For example, `8e9b8aef` exposed the failing finite-index expression in `New.Seesaw.GradingsOfY`, `6bfbe26f` reached the Poincaré series problem, and `53d3fc49` reached `New.Rows.Separation`. ## What Goldbach actually takes from GRH

The compact proof exports five theorems. The final Goldbach bridge uses Mathlib’s `riemannHypothesis` for the Riemann zeta function and `LFunction_eq_zero_re_of_isPrimitive` for primitive Dirichlet characters of positive modulus. In the latter, $0<\Re(s)<1$ and $L(s,\chi)=0$ imply $\Re(s)=1/2$. Both are proved by the compact project and match the argument types in `CompletedFiniteRows`. [Five GRH exports](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/CompactGRH/src/Main.lean) · [Their Mathlib statements](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/CompactGRH/src/Statements.lean) · [Goldbach bridge](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/3ca2fcf7e3dd1897ff242d8e482aa7f82abe263b/CompletedGoldbach.lean)

There is a separate lower-range argument. The final analytic theorem consumes four groups of numerical inequalities and binary Goldbach through **564,383,557,524**. The finite proof checks a contiguous prefix through **564,386,325,504** and uses monotonicity to supply the requested bound. After both branches compiled, the joint Linux run checked the outputs from 794 GRH and 732 finite/analytic modules, compiled the two bridge files, and imported the final theorem in a fresh process. That last operation took 37.321 seconds including the checks and bridges. The old $4\times10^{18}$ binary hypothesis has therefore disappeared from this source proof. The recorded 138 seconds for the GRH tail and 37 seconds for the join measure incremental work. A cold protected run must also rebuild the source, produce the exports and verify the complete solution using the required kernels.
