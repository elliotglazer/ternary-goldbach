# Which theorem each check established

## Statement and toolchains

The November 25 statement experiment used only natural numbers, parity and Mathlib's prime predicate. Its independently written statement says that for every odd natural number greater than five there exist three primes whose sum is that number. Repetition is allowed. Lean 4.33.1 compiled the upstream statement and discharged the equality of the two wrappers by reflexivity. Its two local parameterized checks reported only `propext`. Inspecting the saved release proof gave the expected application and three standard axioms. A direct import of the closed proof could not proceed on that workstation because it lacked the released `Main.olean`. The oldest record thus contains a genuine statement check and a separate readback of an existing artifact. [Pinned historical statement](https://github.com/calr3/ternary-goldbach/blob/23b2d68ca344203d4384e0aff7affa4ec034bef9/TernaryGoldbach/Statement.lean)

On 4.37.0-rc1 the independent challenge compiled. In a small control, the stock comparator accepted an equality proved by reflexivity, accepted it again with a verifier-selected NanoDa, and rejected a different true equality under the same declaration name. Those three controls ran without Bubblewrap. They established that the supplied configuration distinguishes declarations. The 4.37 closed Goldbach import still lacked compatible upstream objects.

Porting the binary sources from 4.33.1 to 4.37.0-rc1 required matching all 415 source hashes, normalizing line endings and giving the sources explicit module headers, public imports and exposed public declarations. Three source changes needed separate treatment. Core had acquired a modular-power implementation, the packed hexadecimal elaborator needed a meta helper, and the factorial proof needed to simplify a local binding before applying induction. A small certificate control exercised two valid cases, rejected three malformed certificates and derived binary Goldbach through 10. This check and the two-block kernel experiment established parts of that version port. The completed project later used Lean 4.35.0.

## Conditional analytic proof and RH inputs

The conditional assembly uses the zeta Riemann hypothesis and critical-line GRH for primitive Dirichlet characters. On the Goldbach candidate the type of `LFunction_eq_zero_re_of_isPrimitive` agrees definitionally with the unbounded primitive-character hypothesis consumed by `CompletedFiniteRows`. A separate two-line Lean implication supplied the older bounded Platt interface. Odd conductors through 300,000 use heights through `100000000/q`. Even conductors through 400,000 use heights through `max (100000000/q) (200 + 75000000/q)`. Both include the height endpoint and concern zeros in the open strip. Full primitive GRH entails both statements by direct specialization. The check also exercised the library's `Fact` wrapper and verified that changing the odd-conductor cutoff breaks the definition comparison. It took the GRH theorem as a hypothesis. ## Locating defects in proof terms

In `ValuationPair`, the elaborator's treatment of a shared default proof affected downstream declarations. The stored original artifacts yielded the immediate reference chain `U.mapEnd → U.map → sorryAx`. The repair elaborated the default separately on its two consumers and named the determinant witness. All 213 exported declarations in the repaired fixture were free of `sorryAx` in their recorded axiom data. The diagnostic reads actual compiled declarations and reports edges from types and values. An empty search frontier can mean an intermediate module was never loaded. The separate final theorem axiom audit supplies the stronger conclusion needed for the release.

Two independent repairs had different causes. `Landau` used a finite-support product and convexity of a ball. The full module and its rectangular-residue consumer compiled. `HHFourier` supplied the null-measurability of the integration interval at two indicator-lemma uses. The complete module compiled in 30.84 seconds. ## Closing the theorem

An early source intake checked 735 Lean files in a 751-file archive, with 357,025,168 submitted bytes. Its independent challenge resolved three sources, namely Init and the Mathlib modules for primes and parity. Later `1b2055cb` bundled the GRH proof, for 1,529 Lean files and 364,780,152 source bytes against the 524,288,000-byte cap. This increase accompanied the decision to submit the upstream mathematical argument as source. Neither file census establishes that the candidate theorem elaborates. The final warm integration checked the successful finite and GRH parents, their Git blobs, direct imports, build traces, toolchain and artifact hashes. All 732 finite input blobs matched across the two source revisions. It compiled the two published adapters, then a fresh Lean process imported the final theorem and printed its type and the three standard axioms. At 11:54:53 UTC this procedure had completed in 37.321 seconds, accounting for 1,526 module traces and 7,630 artifact files. The theorem has no GRH premise. The bridge supplies both RH declarations from the compiled community sources. The separate stock procedure builds an independent randomly named challenge, exports both sides, compares declaration and axiom policy, and invokes independent checkers. Its outcome belongs to its own report.
