# Resources and recovery during the source replays

## What the pilot could test

The simulator checked a fixed, independently sourced challenge in a separate Mathlib-only tree. The protected verifier chose a fresh challenge namespace, audited resolved imports and ran comparator acceptance and rejection controls. Under the original 4.35 policy the repaired simulator and stock selected the same ordered list of 34 export roots and byte-identical trusted challenge text. Both named Lean and NanoDa as mandatory checkers and used the same pinned con-ron override with two replay jobs and a policy for recognized capacity or support declines. Only an actual protected report could establish that the full set of controls passed together. [Historical protected verifier](https://github.com/PalomarRegistry/PalomarSubmission/blob/14aa8d6b3c2f14a8540605e37144560b9a00e7e7/scripts/verify_submission.py)

The larger historical Namespace profile assigned 32 vCPUs and nominally 64 GiB. A 330-minute verification deadline included setup, within a 350-minute workflow limit. At exactly 64 GiB, the stock per-phase memory pressure and hard limits of 95% and 98% give 60.8 and 62.72 GiB. Intake required 20 GiB available disk and at least 60 GiB effective memory for this profile. The simulator recycled trusted archives and periodically removed completed setup files. Stock retained those files. Near a disk limit, the bytes removed by simulator cleanup belong in the estimated stock footprint. Stock clamps later phases to the remaining budget. The pilot could finish a mathematically successful run beyond 330 minutes and label it over budget. ## Measuring the common memory boundary

The first resource test found a gap between a coordinator under a system service and verifier phases launched by a user-manager scope. A 64-GiB limit inherited by just one of them does not measure the combined job. The reviewed arrangement placed both beneath the same measured process-group ancestor. The qualification checked the CPU quota divided by period, 68,719,476,736 bytes of aggregate memory, zero swap, stock's inner thresholds and the actual process memberships. It also checked the initial state and its restoration. An otherwise valid local stock command had earlier stopped at the real 16-CPU capacity limit of its test machine. A larger nominal host alone would not have removed either issue. [Resource and stock replay evidence](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/tree/c6368decde84d4be04943117a2bfef991f9b1831/verification/community-grh/compact/stock-runtime)

An auxiliary design allowed two warm diagnostics with separate ceilings, one of 16 CPUs and 64 GiB and a lower-priority one of 8 CPUs and 40 GiB. It bounded the latter to 35 minutes, required it to drain before releasing the common stock reservation, and required its own terminal evidence. This was a proposed option. The source roots used for the published theorem came from separate successful jobs. The protected run needed exclusive use of its measured resource envelope.

The 6½-hour contingency window preserved the first scheduled stop and required observing the stopped state before opening a new window. A full 350-minute workflow would have had to start by 11:10 UTC to finish at 17:00. A 330-minute local verification alone could have started as late as 11:30. Starting at 10:40 allowed 30 minutes after the nominal workflow for evidence recovery. These were scheduling examples. The final rehearsal used a shorter local cutoff and recorded its original clock before setup. The later published profile offered a 64-vCPU, 512-GiB, 23-hour option subject to maintainer criteria. A failed runtime bootstrap on an otherwise qualifying host did not measure a proof-size rejection. ## Receipts for completed and interrupted work

The early design kept a source checkpoint after upstream GRH compilation and a separate checkpoint after exports. Read-only inputs and hashes covered source identity, compiler, toolchain, build outputs, and the final bridge's expected dependencies. New source builds received independent writable directories. A resumed comparator still had to complete an uninterrupted check on the identified exports. Source logs, complete export hashes, saved successful subproofs and the final kernel verdict answered different questions.

A rolling console buffer sometimes retained a digest and relative reference while losing the full receipt. The improved writer split a compressed receipt into frames of up to 700 characters with a 24-KiB total bundle cap. Its small summary could survive larger full receipts. Recovering a file required reconstructing its original bytes and checking their SHA-256 against the original terminal reference. In the recovery design, historical service output could be read after shutdown if a collector had already obtained it. A stopped host alone produced no new service output. A forced stop could leave source logs and partial artifacts on disk without a terminal marker. [Receipt and source-worker format](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/c6368decde84d4be04943117a2bfef991f9b1831/verification/community-grh/compact/compact-joint-435.md)

On November 26, Ubuntu upgrades restarted the warm supervisor twice, at 06:58:22 and 06:58:26 UTC. The old supervisor had moved both requests from pending to claimed. After restarting, it considered only pending requests and declared itself idle eight minutes later. The private Ruby setup began at 07:02:50, after the restarts. This timing excluded Ruby provisioning as the immediate cause. A regression with five genuinely stale processes showed that deferring one exact proof service preserved the default restart decision for the other four, including a similar name. Removing the rule restored selection of all five. Reconciliation moved a claim with no started directory back to pending and marked a started job lacking completion as interrupted. It preserved the work already on disk.

Warm reuse required the original source and package ancestry, successful Lake records, trace/facet hashes, version pins and an independently writable output. A change to a source invalidated its descendants. A timeout could certify individual completed files where this evidence existed, while the root itself remained unfinished. The interrupted finite build provided 454 such records. One later sealed snapshot admitted 22 completed modules including `WideSource20`, while leaving the unsealed tail ineligible. Subsequent terminal evidence and source-ancestry checks could choose a different subset of that snapshot for a later root. [Warm reuse design](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/tree/c6368decde84d4be04943117a2bfef991f9b1831/verification/community-grh/compact/warm-main/incremental) · [Time-limited source receipts](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/tree/c6368decde84d4be04943117a2bfef991f9b1831/verification/community-grh/compact/warm-main/timed-roots)

Finally, detaching just a long-running phase would still leave its supervising shell responsible for the rest of the sequence. The proposed persistent coordinator covered the clock, qualification, proof and terminal collection together. A disconnect could then be handled by reading the same durable job record. A stop between writing the stock report and the coordinator's authenticated completion marker leaves a raw stock status with uncertain before-cutoff timing. The conservative local classification in that case was inconclusive.
