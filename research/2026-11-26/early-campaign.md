# From QRH to a closed ternary Goldbach theorem, November 10–24

On November 10 the objective was to replace one assumption in Charlie Reams's Lean formalization of Harald Helfgott's proof. On November 24 stock Lean 4.33.1 accepted the unbounded theorem that every odd natural number greater than five is a sum of three primes. The decisive changes were the availability of full Dirichlet GRH, a smaller binary Goldbach cutoff, and a new way of covering the intervening analytic range. The final run imported the released RH and GRH objects. The later effort to compile their source alongside Goldbach and obtain a protected Palomar verdict is a separate campaign. [Original research brief](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/qrh-campaign/BRIEF.md) · [November 24 kernel receipt](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/b37fd3e56064346ff058edb45f81cebf5ce39474/proof/evidence/CompletedGoldbach-receipt.json)

## November 10 · Why replacing the axiom took mathematical work

Elliot selected Reams's `cde-moebius` branch, pinned at `23b2d68c`, as the target. The initial eight research assignments covered zero sums, Mellin bounds, major-arc constants, prime estimates, Möbius estimates, QRH integration, the final statement, and independent feasibility. The large case was to retain its threshold of 10²⁷. The full theorem could initially assume binary Goldbach through 4 × 10¹⁸. The branch already handled the small odd inputs separately from `PlattGRH`. [Pinned Reams source](https://github.com/calr3/ternary-goldbach/tree/23b2d68ca344203d4384e0aff7affa4ec034bef9) · [Assignments and mathematical inputs](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/qrh-campaign/BRIEF.md)

The first obstruction was quantitative. QRH supplied a zero-free region to the right of 7/8. A zero with real part 3/4 would satisfy this information and violate the critical-line premise. At the working scale of 4.9 × 10²⁶, replacing a factor x¹ᐟ² by x⁷ᐟ⁸ makes it about 10.2 billion times as large. The available gap between the major- and minor-arc constants was only 1.049 − 1.0376 = 0.0114. Averaging over characters offered a way to recover cancellation, but the necessary mixed-moment inequality remained open. The prime-estimate researchers had a more tractable subproblem at x ≥ exp(20000) and exp(35000). Lean checked numerical absorption lemmas there. The two full analytic drafts and the closure of the imported QRH provider still awaited validation at the recorded checkpoint. [November 10 calculations and chronology](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-10.md) · [Prime-estimate receipts](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/agent-journal/2026-11-11-prime_estimates.md)

Elliot challenged the first cross-system cost estimates. The figures of 2,500 Lean core-years and 50,000 MM0 core-years both concerned extrapolations to 4 × 10¹⁸. One used an arbitrary formal-verification overhead and the other priced a different proof representation and included generation. Neither supplied an optimized comparison. The smaller endpoint mentioned in discussion, 4 × 10⁸, was eventually covered by an actual Lean theorem through 10⁹ in about two CPU minutes. The team began separating witness search, proof production, proof checking, and the cost of a second independent replay. [Original estimates and corrections](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-10.md) · [Later measured small bounds](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-20.md)

## November 11 · What a QRH blueprint could buy

Elliot asked the team to reduce total verification hours and report estimates for the *whole proof*. Simply replacing `PlattGRH` with another expensive statement earned no saving. He asked the eight agents to concentrate on the blueprint. The main design combined global QRH with stronger finite zero bounds. One stage used β ≤ 0.555 through the smaller of an earlier conductor-dependent height and 10⁸/c. Later trials allowed 0.56 for larger conductors and shortened their height ranges. The original comparison covered 5,303 arithmetic cells. A subsequent high-conductor comparison checked 2,415 cells and 35 combined budgets. The resulting 27.5% reduction was in height-weighted character coverage; no corresponding runtime had been measured. The revised assembly budget reserved 1.0404 for the major term, 1.0376 for the minor term, and 0.00271 for nonprime contributions, leaving 0.00009 in the common normalization. These were conditional budget calculations. [QRH strategy and its hypotheses](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/qrh-blueprint/OVERVIEW.md) · [Dated comparisons and decisions](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-11.md)

The principal character needed special treatment. An early construction required the 1,069 positive-ordinate zeta zeros through height 1,500 on the critical line, an exact count, and a reciprocal sum below 1.668. One sufficient continuation proposed an extremely thin strip up to 10⁶. A later design assumed principal RH through 10⁸ and removed the *separate* height-1,500 count and sum obligations from its selected route. These were successive designs. The finite-family zero proofs, multiplicities, dependencies for prime and Möbius estimates, and the final analytic application remained substantial obligations. Two other much-discussed numbers, 186 and 692, bounded one plus the mean and maximum input magnitudes of a Fourier transform. The former controlled accumulation of rounding error and the latter set integer capacity. Neither located a zero. [Principal moment alternatives](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/qrh-blueprint/next-synthesis/SELECTED_STRATEGY.md) · [Subsequent principal and precision decisions](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-11.md)

## November 12–13 · A certificate benchmark is different from a campaign estimate

Lean checked a binary Goldbach block of 64 even targets near 4 × 10¹⁸, including primality, in 87.8 seconds. A separate positive-height L-function pilot, including its numerical input, tail, Gamma completion and reality condition, took 51.7 seconds. An early model then priced roughly 9.76 × 10¹⁵ invented reference units at 20 ms each plus overhead. This produced 67.7 billion Lean core-hours; a different MM0 model produced 32.7 billion. The pilot did not establish how many such operations an optimized certificate would require. The team withdrew the inference about relative system efficiency. Comparisons subsequently used the same arithmetic workload where possible and stated what a benchmark left out, including formal acceptance of native execution. [Pilot timings and model correction](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-12.md)

HOL4 became a serious alternative. The team measured the cost of moving numerical arrays into theorem representations and of running arithmetic inside the checker. A matched batch of 8,192 arithmetic operations took about 74 ms in HOL and 3.75 ms in native GMP, excluding input translation. HOL4 subsequently checked a theorem giving the actual raw logarithm routine an error of at most 2⁻¹²⁸ under its input conditions. Later kernel exports included sign, parity and Turing-count components. Formal equivalence between the sample values and the relevant L-functions, and a campaign-wide bound on the cost of repairing missed sign changes, still mattered. The archive contains 52 selected HOL4 theory exports with three available local parents. [Raw-log result](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-13.md) · [HOL4 export inventory](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/hol-exports/README.md)

Three historical workloads must stay separate. The binary Goldbach computation through 4 × 10¹⁸ occupied about 6.85 million single-core hours, including repeated checks. Platt's complete numerical GRH run took roughly 400,000 core-hours; Helfgott retrospectively allocated about 160,000 to his required subset. The November 13 HOL4 full-proof planning estimate was 4.58 million hours. Later measured panels brought one proposed fused numerical subtotal to 783,638–795,534 hours, still excluding some analytic work, repairs, proof transport and the finite Goldbach component. The apparent near-2-to-1 ratio compares different scopes and hardware. The broader archive also retains Candle, Rocq, Lean, MM0 and verified-native experiments. ## November 20 · Full GRH changed the small-input problem

Elliot chose to pursue the GRH-based 10²⁰ route after pointing the team to Levent Alpöge's work. A 4.33.1 release supplied the statements about zeta and primitive Dirichlet characters that the Goldbach proof could use. Andrew Sutherland documented a source build, module replay, independent checks of the export, and a statement comparison of the Dirichlet theorem. Integrating the Goldbach code across different Lean versions remained necessary. The mathematical benefit of full GRH was larger than the finite substitution alone. A prime close to an odd target leaves a much smaller even remainder, reducing the largest binary Goldbach certificate required. [Contemporaneous GRH import audit](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/grh-goldbach-20261120/team/grh-build.md) · [Independent verification archive](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

The team investigated Zinoviev's conditional theorem near 10²⁰, Schoenfeld's explicit theta estimate, Dudek's prime-gap estimate, and a localized explicit formula. For the 10²⁷ large-case fallback, initial binary endpoints were 10¹⁶ with Schoenfeld and 4 × 10¹⁵ with the localized argument. The latter needed careful control of prime powers and endpoints. An arithmetic check found 33 exceptions above 2,330 to a totient estimate used in the Zinoviev analysis; the last was 3,990. The repaired inequality held from 3,991, leaving the downstream modulus and arc budgets to check. Four technical exchanges with Claude record the prime-interval proposal, the prime-power objection, the Gaussian zero sum, and review of the large-case assembly. The next accepted contiguous prefix reached 10,134,217,728. Two separate 134,217,728-even sample blocks near 6.12 × 10¹¹ and 10¹² later informed projections; a high-end sample does not establish coverage below it. ## November 20–21 · The budget set the order of work

The team compared the cost of reaching the original binary bound with the time available for a weekend run. Even with 300 comparable cores at 70% utilization, a projected 51,500 useful core-hours would take more than ten days once the checker was ready. The 10²⁰ route had a much smaller forecast for its binary portion, but two analytic arguments had no measured build. The team prioritized a large odd-input theorem and, at Elliot's request, measurable contiguous binary prefixes. This chronology explains earlier low odds for Monday and why those forecasts were a poor guide to a later proof with a different cutoff. [November 21 route and calendar comparisons](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/97a45946f7d31239ecd466e637116a774f24b7d3/work/publish-handoffs/research-journal/2026-11-21.md)

The six finite bands reached 100,328,530,944. Band 06 alone added 16,374,562,816 even targets, with 1,117.375 CPU seconds to produce witnesses and 3,379.1875 CPU seconds to verify them. The source/object fingerprints and exported axioms were saved at each join. Another retained strategy used 254,638,308 windows in the existing prime ladder; reducing its binary premise would require shorter gaps or a denser ladder. The large-case backport passed genuine submodules including `XiPartial` and `WeilLines`. At the November 22 archive inspection the final large-case application was still outstanding. ## November 24 · What finally closed the theorem

The successful construction used a handoff at 2 × 10¹⁹. Its RH prime-window rule allowed any even binary cutoff B ≥ 100,328,530,944 satisfying 126.2√H + 2 ≤ B for the chosen ternary height H ≤ 10²⁷. At H = 2 × 10¹⁹, the proof requests a cutoff of 564,383,557,524. The constructed contiguous binary theorem ends slightly higher at 564,386,325,504. The finite proof includes the small inputs and the prime-gap reduction to an even remainder. Four groups of minor-arc inequalities cover 223 analytic intervals; a uniform argument handles the infinite tail when log n ≥ 56. The statistical forecasts for a large Platt recalculation ceased to describe the proof that actually ran. [Prime-window rule](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/b37fd3e56064346ff058edb45f81cebf5ce39474/proof/FiniteAtHeight.lean) · [Finite and analytic assembly](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/b37fd3e56064346ff058edb45f81cebf5ce39474/proof/CompletedFiniteRows.lean) · [Proof guide](https://github.com/elliotglazer/ternary-goldbach/blob/b37fd3e56064346ff058edb45f81cebf5ce39474/proof/README.md)

The development record distinguishes candidate generation from acceptance. For example, Arb screened 12,645 boxes with a maximum reported upper bound of 0.31449707 against a target of 0.315. Those values guided the analytic proof. The four completed Lean groups supply the final certificates. A different experiment at 4 × 10¹⁸ ran a native coverage check on 68,719,476,736 even targets and obtained a closed kernel theorem only for its last 262,144-even tile. That experiment was never a full kernel proof through 4 × 10¹⁸ and was unnecessary for the released theorem.
