# How the analytic proof reached the finite check

On November 26, 2026, stock Lean 4.35.0 on Linux compiled the theorem that every odd natural number greater than five is a sum of three primes. A fresh Lean process printed its type and the three axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The proof takes over from a finite check at $2\cdot10^{19}$. Earlier plans used $10^{27}$ and $10^{20}$. The change in cutoff explains much of the difference in the projected cost. This chapter records how the project chose it, what the analytic proof needs, and how the pieces were checked. [Source and build record][joined] · [Exact theorem output][log]

## October 19 and November 10–11. What would have to replace Platt?

Elliot Glazer initially pointed to Steve Hubbard's Goldbach Lean project, then identified Charlie Reams's `cde-moebius` branch as the proof whose hypothesis he wanted to remove. The distinction matters for attribution. Hubbard's group formalized published results around Goldbach, including work on the ternary theorem for sufficiently large odd integers. Reams formalized Harald Helfgott's explicit proof. The Glazer project ultimately reused parts of both developments. An October 19 source file in Hubbard's project already contained an example of why a paper-level shortcut could be dangerous. Claude, working in Hubbard's lab, checked that $q=2730$ violates the printed totient inequality in Zinoviev's argument. Here $\varphi(2730)=576$. At $N=10^{21}$ the same modulus violates the claimed principal-term estimate in the indicated range. The file also separates the final arithmetic of the circle argument from the analytic inequalities on which it depends. The final source manifest credits Hubbard's group for this `Arith.lean` file and the three Saouter modules `Theorem2`, `Paper`, and `ModPow`. [Hubbard's arithmetic and counterexamples][arith] · [Source manifest][manifest]

The November target was Reams's source at `23b2d68c`. [Hubbard's project][hubbard] · [Reams's hypothesis inventory][reams] · [Source notices][notice]

Reams's end theorem already had a three-axiom report. It also had two mathematical premises. `PlattGRH` places the nontrivial zeros of primitive Dirichlet L-functions on the critical line over explicit finite ranges. For odd conductor $q\le300{,}000$, the formal statement requires height $10^8/q$. For even conductor $q\le400{,}000$, it uses the larger of $10^8/q$ and $200+7.5\cdot10^7/q$. The case $q=1$ supplies a bound for the zeros of zeta through height $10^8$. `BinaryGoldbachInput` supplies two primes for every even integer from 4 through $4\cdot10^{18}$. A checked ladder of Proth primes uses that premise to cover the odd integers through $10^{27}$. A type such as `PlattGRH → Statement` can have the ordinary three logical axioms even while the project still owes a proof of `PlattGRH`. [Original statement][origstatement] · [Original dependencies and ladder][reams]

The QRH theorem in OpenAI's pinned mathematics repository excluded zeros to the right of $7/8$. The obstruction shows up in a single term of the explicit formula. A zero with real part $\beta$ contributes a factor of size $x^\beta$ before smoothing and summation. Replacing $1/2$ by $7/8$ multiplies this factor by $x^{3/8}$, already above $10^{10}$ at $x=10^{27}$. Increasing $x$ eventually makes either power small compared with the main term of order $x$. Our checked `qrh_low_rpow_absorb` gives one such estimate starting at $\exp(35000)$. It leaves the original starting range uncovered. The same Platt premise also fed bounds for Chebyshev's $\psi$ and $\theta$, through to Mertens estimates and the minor-arc base gap. [QRH source][qrh] · [First QRH experiments][first] · [Reams's dependency map][reams]

Eight agents investigated whether damping, zero counts, symmetry and better allocations of error could compensate. One principal-character calculation paired zeros by conjugation and reflection and bounded their mass using the logarithmic derivative of the completed zeta function at 2. Across 2,750 finite-height cells, its largest rigorously enclosed ratio was 83.571300906 against a target of 85. An independent higher-precision replay checked the final inequalities. Those results came from external interval arithmetic. The compiled Lean results at that checkpoint comprised two numerical absorption lemmas and a Mellin-transform estimate. Moving the interval calculation into Lean and assembling the remaining character estimates were still open. [Calculation, source reviews and Lean receipts][first]

The next blueprint shows the cost of keeping the original $10^{27}$ architecture. The agents proposed bounds such as $\beta\le111/200$ on a conductor-dependent finite family. For the principal character the plan included all 1,069 positive-ordinate zeros through height 1,500, their reciprocal sum, and a much narrower strip above them through height $10^6$. The resulting comparison for the original representation sum allocated 1.0404 to the major lower bound, 1.0376 to the minor upper bound and 0.00271 to nonprime contributions. The surplus was 0.00009 in units of $x^2/49$. The plan identifies each inequality and its remaining obligations. It records how much finite accuracy the zero-free strip left to recover. [Selected strategy and certificate obligations][blueprint]

By the November 11 implementation decision the team had also costed a route that kept restricted finite RH as the primary plan and made QRH optional. It reduced the proposed nonprincipal family to odd conductors through 132,900 and conductors divisible by four through 265,800. On 125,400 saved conductor rows, two often-quoted numbers applied at different steps. A bound of 186 controlled error amplification in the precision assignment. A pointwise bound of 692 sized the intermediate operands. The revised assignment saved five or six precision bits per row under its stated assumptions, but it also increased the number of distinct cached entries. It produced neither L-values nor a measured total-time saving. This was a reason to keep the sign and completeness proofs in the cost estimate. [November 11 implementation decision][decision]

## November 12–22. Change the target before paying for the original range

One alternative was to reconstruct Platt's verification with certifying code. The HOL4 investigation reached actual proofs about sign scans, interval arithmetic and counts conditional on the supplied signs. At conductor 10,000 it reproduced four sign streams comprising 339,562 signs. A complete proof still needed the analytic relation between those signs and the Hardy functions, certification across the conductor range, and the Turing count proving no zeros had been missed. That work clarified which parts an efficient checker could share. It also showed why replaying a sign archive alone could not discharge Reams's premise. [Sign certification handoff][signs] · [Turing and prime-gap handoff][gaphandoff]

When Levent Alpöge's GRH release became available, the group pursued two consequences separately. Full GRH implies the finite zero locations in `PlattGRH` by restriction. The adapter team compared the primitive Dirichlet statement in the released proof export with the proposition used by the adapter. The existing consumer, however, had a different Lean/Mathlib toolchain and still called the large finite binary theorem for small arguments. By the November 22 handoff, the 4.33.1 backport had compiled genuine Fourier and explicit-formula dependencies. It had split the Mertens calculation through 10,000 into 320 intervals and the totient-sum calculation at 150,000 into smaller proofs. The combined `PsiSieve3` proof had hit the kernel recursion limit after its eight block equalities had passed. The handoff distinguished each successful target from the outstanding assembly. [Release adapter and backport record][largeplan] · [November 22 handoff][handoff]

The other path used D. Zinoviev's GRH-based circle-method argument as a starting point. Glazer selected the $10^{20}$ route, recorded in the November 20 plan. The plan estimated that an RH prime-gap bound could reduce the binary requirement to 1,615,000,000,000. That figure was a design input. The team then found a numerical obstacle in the supporting totient argument. The inequality $\varphi(q)>14.1\sqrt q$ fails at 33 integers above 2,330, the last at 3,990. The repair note proves it for $q\ge3991$ and identifies what the excluded values cost at the critical height. The final code uses certified weaker inequalities where the proof consumes them. Examples include $120q<\varphi(q)^2$ for $q\ge2331$, and $99q<\varphi(q)^2$ for $q\ge2100$ in the lower range. [November 20 plan][plan] · [Zinoviev's paper][zinpaper] · [Totient audit][totientnote] · [Lower-range Lean theorem][totient]

The finite pilot also changed the decision. An experiment near $4\cdot10^{18}$ performed a native cover of a block of 68,719,476,736 even targets and kernel-checked its terminal tile of 262,144 targets. The receipt distinguishes those scopes. Certifying the entire interval through $4\cdot10^{18}$ still required the rest of the prefix. Lowering the starting height of the analytic proof would instead reduce the size of the binary theorem itself. [Endpoint experiment][endpoint]

## The analytic argument used in the release

For a target integer $N$, the proof defines $a_N(n)=\Lambda(n)e^{-n/N}$ and the wave $S_N(\alpha)=\sum_n a_N(n)e(n\alpha)$, where $e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)$. Fourier orthogonality identifies the real part of the integral of $S_N(\alpha)^3 e(-N\alpha)$ over one period with the sum of $a_N(i)a_N(j)a_N(k)$ over $i+j+k=N$. The von Mangoldt function is supported on prime powers. A positive coefficient could therefore come from higher powers. The proof bounds their total using $\psi-\theta$. If the major-arc lower bound exceeds both the minor-arc contribution and this prime-power allowance, at least one contributing triple consists of primes. Reams's circle-method code supplies the underlying Fourier identity. [Wave and convergence][wave] · [Coefficient identity and prime extraction][coefficient]

**Major arcs.** Near a rational $a/q$ with small denominator, the proof compares the wave integral with its principal-character model. For odd $N\ge2\cdot10^{19}$, the truncated Ramanujan sum has real part at least 1.25. After the factor $1/(2e)$, its leading coefficient exceeds 0.22983. Truncation and integration errors of at most $0.00112N^2$ leave the restricted model above $0.2287N^2$. The final numerical estimate puts the major integral at least as high as the model minus $0.0032N^2$. The prime-extraction lemma allows a loss of $0.005N^2$. This slack makes the numerical threshold easier to check. [Model lower bound][model] · [Major-arc comparison][major]

**Minor arcs.** For each remaining $\alpha$, rational approximation yields a denominator $q$ and a scaled displacement $\delta$. The proof estimates the principal part and the twisted-character remainder, on the same wave and at the same point. In the explicit formula the primitive character controls the location of the nontrivial zeros. Keeping the sign of the zero ordinate gives stronger gamma-factor decay on one half-line. A bound on the supremum of the wave, together with an integral of $|S_N|^2$, bounds the cubic minor-arc integral. The required supremum is $0.315N/\log N$. In the lower range the coefficient comparison uses $0.705\cdot0.315+0.001=0.223075$, whereas the model can afford $0.2287-0.005=0.2237$. The remaining margin is positive. These inequalities refer to the actual exponentially smoothed wave used in the Fourier identity. [Character explicit formula][char] · [Minor-arc estimate][minor] · [Comparison at the lower cutoff][model]

There are two ways to pay the numerical error in this argument. For $\log N\ge56$, monotone estimates prove both budgets uniformly. For $2\cdot10^{19}\le N$ and $\log N<56$, the major estimate still holds uniformly, while the minor estimate uses verified interval tables. The four table families come from the split at $N=10^{20}$ and at $q=10^8$. Below $10^{20}$ the rational-approximation order is $30^2(\log N)^4$. Above it the order is $70^2(\log N)^4$. The tabulated log intervals $[44.44,46.052]$ and $[46.05,56]$ overlap, so there is room for the comparison at $\log(10^{20})$. Large $|\delta|$ is handled separately. Each table certifies an inequality for a whole parameter interval, which lets four finite objects bound a continuum of arc locations. [Uniform tail][tail] · [Bounded major estimate][bounded] · [Four table families and coverage proof][profiles]

The four `PriceRows` families have 56, 102, 14 and 51 pieces, respectively. In Lean, `ternary_of_profiles` takes the four proved families, binary Goldbach through 564,383,557,524, RH for zeta, and GRH for primitive Dirichlet characters. It applies the bounded minor estimate to each odd input in the remaining range. This gave the analytic and finite agents a shared theorem against which to test their work. During the 4.35 port the analytic team caught a real induction case-name change in `ScaledEval`. One interval checker also needed its 657-entry computation broken into smaller checked pieces to keep its memory requirements manageable. The numeric controls and final Linux receipts distinguish a successful individual profile from compilation of the full theorem. [Profile assembly][assembly] · [Numerical source checks][numericchecks] · [657-entry check][check657]

## Why binary Goldbach through 564 billion suffices

For the finite branch let $H=2\cdot10^{19}$ and put $x=n-4$. For $x$ between $10^{15}$ and $10^{27}$, the RH prime-in-interval lemma supplies a prime $p$ just below $x$ with window width $126.2\sqrt{x}$. Then $n-p$ is at least 4 and even. The finite argument includes a separate small-case proof for $n\le10^{15}+4$. Its general `through_height_of_rh` theorem requires an even $B\ge100{,}328{,}530{,}944$ and $126.2\sqrt H+2\le B$. The extra two units allow the final rounding and parity argument. Lean uses $\sqrt{2\cdot10^{19}}\le4{,}472{,}135{,}955$ to prove the condition for $B=564{,}383{,}557{,}524$. [Finite-height lemma][finiteheight] · [Threshold arithmetic][lowercut]

The arithmetic certificate reaches the even endpoint 564,386,325,504. Monotonicity supplies the slightly smaller prefix the proof consumes. Thus the binary computation ends about 35 million times below the lower endpoint of the large ternary theorem. A prime chosen close to each odd $n$ makes that possible. Nothing in this step assumes binary Goldbach at arbitrarily large values. [Completed finite proof][finiteassembly] · [Finite source closure and receipt][finitereceipt]

## November 25–26. Joining the hypotheses to their proofs

The archived Lean 4.33.1 release had already compiled `CompletedGoldbach.ternary_goldbach` using the released RH and GRH objects. Its receipt names the compiled object, statement hash and three axioms. The replay used those objects as inputs. A complete source replay had a different engineering requirement. The Lean 4.35 port paired the same mathematical argument with Sernau's compact packaging of Levent Alpöge's proof with Claude, taken from the community mirror. The package contains 794 source modules. Historical notices preserve Reams's and Hubbard's contributions and the contribution of the PrimeNumberTheoremAnd project to Reams's preliminary estimates. [Original 4.33.1 receipt][release] · [Compact upstream tree][compact] · [Attribution and source pins][notice]

The final application is short because the earlier lemmas already have the exact interfaces. `CompletedFiniteRows` supplies the binary prefix and all four profile families. Its theorem still takes two explicit arguments. The module `Main` exports `riemannHypothesis` at the root namespace. The second root declaration, `LFunction_eq_zero_re_of_isPrimitive`, applies in the open strip $0<\Re(s)<1$ to a primitive character at nonzero natural modulus and gives $\Re(s)=1/2$ for any zero there. The zeta statement is used for the short prime interval as well as the large-number estimates. `CompletedGoldbach` passes the two actual theorems into `completed_of_release`. The wrapper spells out the public natural-number statement. Its type has no premise about zeros, finite verification or prime gaps. [Compact GRH exports][main] · [Closed application][bridge] · [Public theorem][solution]

The November 26 Linux runs compiled 13 remaining GRH modules with 781 available, and 207 remaining finite-and-analytic modules with 525 available. The latter sources agreed at every one of the 732 finite-root module paths between the finite run and the joined candidate. The join checked the retained outputs of all 1,526 modules, compiled both bridge files, then imported the theorem in a fresh process and printed its axioms. Those last steps took 37.321 seconds altogether. That time measures the final join with the two compiled roots present. A cold build, independent protected replay of the complete proof, and Registry acceptance each have their own evidence requirements.
