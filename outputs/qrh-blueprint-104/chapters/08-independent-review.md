## Invalid shortcuts ruled out by the combined documents

Second moments alone cannot prove the mixed target. On a set of measure epsilon take nonnegative energies Eplus=A/epsilon and Estar=B/epsilon, zero elsewhere. Their integrals are A and B while the integral of their product is AB/epsilon. The concentration problem survives character orthogonality unless the retained arithmetic or spectral structure supplies additional control.

The exact Gauss weights, including zero support for excluded imprimitive characters, must survive every expansion. Aplus/Astar's squarefree support does not restrict Cross or upper N to squarefree moduli. Replacing the two independent characters in Cross by one diagonal character changes the quantity. Similarly, a global first zero-density bound does not control the same-modulus product of two cell counts without a local bound or pair-density estimate.

The four-zero operator route correctly makes the x-dependent phases diagonal and the amplitude factors contractions once the QRH strip holds. An oscillatory numerical result at x0, by itself, has no monotonic extension to all larger x. Operator or absolute-envelope bounds can supply such an extension. The zero-sum chapter also correctly rejects an entrywise comparison between a restricted-interval kernel and its full-line kernel.

The model density inequality N(s)<=C*B^(a*(1-s)) remains an explicitly hypothetical interface. Choosing a=12/5 in a sensitivity calculation does not provide that theorem, its effective C, treatment of real zeros, or its ranges. A finite beta<3/4 certificate could help small conductors, but the current blueprint supplies neither a sufficient cutoff nor the certificate. Exporting either inequality as a new root premise would leave the requested proof incomplete.

For minor arcs, a bound valid from exp(20000) cannot repair a consumer quantified from 1e12. The endpoints below 1e9 can use existing finite source proofs. Every remaining interval needs an explicit overlap argument. Integrating a zero expansion can improve its weight, but Abel summation and unsmoothing can remove the gain. The prime chapter correctly leaves the E1 edges open.

## Appendix on a standard-axioms-only finite theorem

This is the user's secondary question and requires no work tonight. The existing `Helfgott/SmallCases.lean:21` proves `TernaryGoldbachUpTo (10^27)` with `Fact BinaryGoldbachInput`. For n<=4e18+3 it uses 3 plus a binary representation of n-3. Above that it chooses rung floor((n-5)/3927138875067072512), obtains a prime p with n-4e18<=p<=n-4, and applies the same binary input to n-p. A standard-axioms-only theorem via this route therefore needs a checked proof of BG4e18, plus validation of the existing ladder proof.

The ladder is already structured as compressed exact checking. `Ladder/Check.lean` packs 11-bit offsets, checks blocks of 32 candidates modulo their product, and groups 128 blocks per chunk. `Ladder/Data.lean:138` states `RunsOK 1 62168`, covering 254640128 checked rungs and 7957504 blocks. The public ladder theorem uses 254638308 rungs. The raw packed hints for the checked rungs total 350130176 bytes before proof-object or serialization overhead. Hints are generated during elaboration and checked by `decide +kernel`; arbitrary hints cannot bypass the proved checker.

The upstream README, lines 119-129, reports about 1.5e-5 seconds per rung for hint search, 5.5e-5 seconds for kernel checking, and five one-core hours overall, of which four are checking. These are source-reported historical measurements, with hardware details absent from that paragraph. This review did not benchmark them. The README's 1.7 GB per file wording should not be converted into a total persistent-artifact size without measurement. [Pinned README](https://github.com/calr3/ternary-goldbach/blob/23b2d68ca344203d4384e0aff7affa4ec034bef9/README.md#L119)

One source comment is numerically wrong. `Ladder/Main.lean` says rung 254638308 lies above 10^27. Its upper endpoint is actually 999999998428102730968989697, below 10^27 by 1571897269031010303. The floor-based small-case proof needs a prime below n and is consistent with this number. This is a documentation correction, with no proof failure established here.

The binary computation has a different scale. Oliveira e Silva, Herzog and Pardi report about 770 one-core CPU years for their verification through 4e18, including computation of minimal partitions and statistics. That historical optimized computation gives no Lean runtime prediction. Their paper reports a direct ternary corollary only through 8.37e26 using a different prime-gap result. [Primary paper, pages 2033-2034](https://denisevellachemla.eu/empirical-verification-of-the-even-CG.pdf)

There are exactly 1999999999999999999 even integers in [4,4e18]. A literal one-bit result per even integer is about 250 decimal petabytes, and one byte of witness information per integer is about two exabytes. These are storage costs of those naive formats, not information-theoretic lower bounds. Streaming avoids storing the whole interval, while a verified block sieve and bitset coverage checker could amortize primality and representation checks. Such a checker would still need complete coverage, exact arithmetic, and a measured throughput plan. Checksums or summary statistics alone do not prove that every even number has a representation.

Compressed alternatives include certified blocks of prime bits with a verified sieve, a small-prime shift cover with a proved bitset union, and shared primality certificates for reused witnesses. They can reduce redundancy. No supplied algorithm or benchmark establishes a practical kernel checking cost for the full BG range. A cryptographic succinct proof would introduce additional soundness assumptions unless its conclusion is reconstructed by an exact proof-producing checker.

An alternative reduction could lower the binary threshold and use a denser ladder, or prove ternary coverage directly on blocks. Lowering the binary bound increases the required prime coverage approximately as 10^27 divided by the allowed gap. For example a uniform gap around 1e12 would need on the order of 1e15 intervals, so replacing BG4e18 with a much smaller verification is not automatically cheaper. A successful analytic argument covering the middle range might change this tradeoff, but it would constitute an additional mathematical result. The current finite proof gives a precise reduction and a reusable ladder; it supplies no unconditional BG certificate yet.

### Tunable binary threshold and a denser ladder

There is a rigorous generic reduction. Let B>=4 be even and let C be the finite ternary endpoint. Suppose binary Goldbach holds for every even m in [4,B]. Suppose 3=p0<p1<...<pk are odd primes, every adjacent difference is at most B-2, and pk+B is at least the largest odd integer at most C. Then every odd n in [7,C] is the sum of three primes. Indeed each pj covers the odd integers in [pj+4,pj+B]. The first interval starts at7, adjacent intervals leave no missing odd integer because pj+1+4<=pj+B+2, and the last interval reaches the endpoint. For any covered n, n-pj is an even integer in [4,B]. This is a proof of a reduction; BG and the prime coverage remain inputs to discharge.

For window certificates with pj in [Lj,Uj], the conservative sufficient adjacent condition is Uj+1-Lj<=B-2. The endpoint can use Lk+B. Alternatively certify the actual prime gaps, which can save the worst-case window slack. All integer endpoints and the odd parity restriction are part of the coverage check.

Write r=B/(4e18), H=770*365.25*24=6749820 hours, and L=5 hours. Under the purely illustrative cost model

    T(r)=H*r+L/r,

the minimum is at r=sqrt(L/H)=.00086067444, so B is about3.4427e15. Both terms would then cost about5809.4 hours, for a total11618.8 hours or1.3254 core-years. A continuum model uses about2.9047e11 prime intervals at that B. The present ladder already pays a small window-slack factor, so scaling its exact rung count by1/r is slightly larger. This model is useful for demonstrating a tradeoff and selecting ranges for later measurements. It supplies no implementation runtime forecast.

The constants come from different tasks and execution systems. Historical H is optimized native computation of minimal Goldbach partitions, sieving, and statistics. L includes a particular native hint search and a particular kernel checker on fixed Proth candidates. A future BG checker may omit minimality and statistics, but may add expensive proof checking; its cost could change in either direction. Bit lengths, sieve setup, exceptional gaps, proof-object handling, and parallel scaling also vary with B. A meaningful optimum needs measured a(B) and c(B), with search, certificate generation, kernel checking, memory, and storage reported separately.

The present ladder cannot be made that dense by changing its spacing constant alone. Its candidate lattice step is F=2^45=35184372088832 and its permitted offset width is2047F=72022409665839104. At the illustrative optimum B/F is only97.85. The existing worst-case window guarantee (S+2047)F<=B-2 therefore cannot hold for any positive S. Shrinking the offset range would require new evidence that every shorter window contains a certifiable prime. The current search also demands c mod3=2 and uses base3, making this an even more specific candidate family.

There are additional concrete code constraints. The current checker and proof use a28-bit rung index and enforce i<2^28, while the denser model needs about2.9e11 rungs. Those loops and proofs would need wider index bounds. The existing Proth argument needs c<(2^45+1)^2, which still accommodates the endpoint near1e27; decreasing the lattice exponent far enough to gain density loses this particular uniform primality criterion. A general Pocklington or other certified primality argument would be a new component.

A better exploratory design is an adaptive ladder with its actual gaps checked. Use inexpensive Proth witnesses where available, and certify additional primes by a general method only in exceptional gaps. In parallel, design BG verification as blockwise existence coverage with shared prime sieving; minimal partitions are unnecessary. These choices target avoidable work on each side of the model. Their practical merit depends on measuring a small, representative high-end block and the worst required gap behavior. No prototype, benchmark, or new certificate was run for this follow-up.
